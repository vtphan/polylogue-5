## Authorial notes

Episode 6 is a scope-primary episode whose scope-carrier is
deliberately absent. That is the structural move. The arc cannot have
Priya deliver the scope inflection in ep 7 as a clean reveal if the
scope problem has not been *half-present* in ep 6 with Priya absent;
conversely the inflection will not land if the scope problem has
been *fully named* without her. The needle this episode threads is
*visible-but-unnamed*: the reader sees the scope hole clearly on the
page (the south-side clustering, the words Maya cannot quite find,
Cam's honest pushback) while the characters cannot fully see it from
inside the data. That is what `stalemate` means here — not a
dramatic impasse but a scene that cannot resolve itself because the
tool it needs is missing.

Three leads, not four. Priya's absence is load-bearing and should be
planted off-handedly: a mention in the cold open that her mom had
something at her aunt's, nothing more. Dramatizing the absence would
telegraph what ep 7 is supposed to land cleanly. The design doc
flags ep 6 as a `scaffolding_id` calibration target precisely because
of this — hints for ep 6 have to direct attention toward *whose walk
is missing* without naming `condition_sensitivity` outright.

On Cam as the weakness carrier: the arc summary says *the group is
tempted to call the mystery solved* without naming who speaks the
closing line. Assigning the weakness to Cam is the best choice for
three reasons. First, it continues Cam's five-episode pattern shape
in a new register without planting a new cognitive pattern — the
move is *a claim/data exists, therefore it counts*, the same habit
he has exhibited with a TikTok (ep 1), Jules's retelling of Anya
(ep 2), and his own post's replies (ep 3), now applied to a map.
Second, it gives the episode a natural `perspective_engagement`
weakness carrier while Priya — the character who will later widen
the frame — is absent. Cam is speaking from inside the group's own
walk and mistaking that shared viewpoint for the whole park. Third,
it avoids putting the weakness on Maya in the same episode where her
proto-strength needs to land cleanly — a weakness-then-strength arc
within one character across one episode would crowd her.

On Cam's weakness having no named cognitive pattern: his three
declared patterns (`uncritical_acceptance`, `overgeneralization`,
`confirmation_bias`) have all planted by ep 3, and the ep 6 move is
habit-level rather than fallacy-shaped. The same choice was made
for Priya in ep 1 and ep 4 (her condition_sensitivity moves are
facet-level with no pattern). Treating condition_sensitivity as a
habit that can live on multiple characters without cognitive-pattern
labeling is pedagogically the right move: it tells students *this
kind of mistake is not owned by one character, it is a shape of
thinking anyone can fall into*. Planning_agent should read the
cognitive_signal as the full behavioral trace for Cam's weakness;
the beats reinforce the shape physically (the marker on the map,
the confident *so this is the mystery*).

On Maya's strength facet being `reasoning_completeness` and not
`condition_sensitivity`: the move is *the answer does not close the
question I asked* — a completeness audit on the investigation's own
opening question. Calling it condition_sensitivity would force the
insight into the precise language Priya is supposed to arrive at in
ep 7 (*we were drawing a map of the part of the park we walk*) and
would therefore blow the ep-7 inflection. Maya's move stays
deliberately one step short of that naming: she notices that the
answer does not close the question (completeness), but she does
not yet name *why* the answer falls short in scope-vocabulary terms
(condition_sensitivity). That is exactly the line between a
proto-strength and an inflection, and the ep-6 strength needs to
land on the proto side of it. Reviewers should read Maya's half-
sentence — *if the map just shows where we've walked, then maybe we
just know where they are where we've been looking* — as awkward by
design, not polished; the awkwardness is the point.

On Maya carrying strength in a sixth consecutive episode: this is
within the design-doc's explicit commitment to Maya's arc (ep 5
inflection, ep 9 second chain, ep 10 formalizing-by-default) and it
matches her characterization as the kid who notices what is
incomplete. The rotation rule is not violated across the full
season — Jules carries engine-state-not-growth and therefore carries
weakness episodes without strength episodes, Cam carries his own
strength beats in eps 2, 5, and 8, and Priya carries her inflection
in ep 7 and growing strengths in the back half. The season-level
balance is intact; Maya's load reflects her being the Logic grower
in a story where Logic lands in four episodes.

On Jules in ep 6: no designed target, similar to her ep-3 and ep-4
roles. Her presence is emotionally textured — she feels the map's
clean cluster as relief because a closed mystery is a dissolved
fear. When Maya rereads the Monday question and keeps the notebook
open, Jules does not push back (she is not ready to close, at some
level, and she is tired). The dialog writer should give Jules one
or two quiet lines and let her be the emotional temperature of the
scene without forcing a target move on her. Adding another Jules
weakness in a sixth consecutive episode would dilute her pattern;
letting her be the *relief-and-tiredness* presence lets ep 7's
north-side walk feel like a shared re-engagement after a stalemate.

No social dynamics in this episode. The scene is cooperative and
there is no group_pressure beat to stage — nobody is pressing Jules
to agree with anything, nobody is softening an objection, the
disagreement between Cam's closure move and Maya's half-insight is
flat and not interpersonally freighted. `authority_deference` is
dormant and will not return until ep 8. Adding a social dynamic
here would over-engineer a scene whose structural work is already
carrying a lot (absence-as-scope-problem, temptation-to-close,
half-naming).

## Why these targets

**condition_sensitivity / Cam / (facet-level).** The arc summary
names ep 6 as the episode where *the group is tempted to call the
mystery solved* and *the scope problem is on the page and nobody
quite names it.* The temptation to close must have a voice, and
Cam's five-episode pattern — claim-exists-therefore-claim-counts —
is the natural voice for it, rotated from Evidence to scope. The
facet `condition_sensitivity` fits precisely: Cam is reading the
cluster's cleanness as if it were a property of the squirrels' world
rather than a property of the group's walking routes. No cognitive
pattern is named because his declared patterns have all planted by
ep 3 and forcing one here would sharpen the signal in the wrong
direction — the move is habit-level, not fallacy-shaped, and the
story's design commits to treating scope weaknesses as facet-level
habits (see Priya in eps 1 and 4).

**reasoning_completeness (strength) / Maya.** The arc summary
explicitly names Maya as the resistance-to-close carrier: *Maya
resists by rereading the original question she wrote in episode 1
and noticing that the question was broader than where do red-eyed
squirrels live in the part of the park we walk.* The facet is
`reasoning_completeness` rather than `condition_sensitivity` because
Maya's move is *the answer does not close the opening question* —
a completeness check on the investigation's arc — rather than a
full scope audit naming why. Keeping the facet distinct from Priya's
ep-7 facet is load-bearing for the arc: ep 6 must leave room for
ep 7 to *name* what ep 6 has only *gestured at*. This is Maya's
third `reasoning_completeness` strength (ep 1, ep 4, ep 6), and the
repetition is deliberate — she is the character who persistently
notices what is incomplete, across varied contexts, and the season
trains the reader to associate her with that move.

## Calibration note for this episode

The register is cooler than any previous episode. The alien-anxiety
peaked in ep 3 and has been receding since ep 5's chain closed;
ep 6's emotional work is a *tempted closure*, where the tiredness
and relief of possibly being done hangs over the scene. The dialog
writer should render Maya's temptation as *real* — she genuinely
wants to put the pen down, and for a beat she does — and her
decision to reread the question as *not heroic* but quiet, almost
reluctant. The half-naming must stay awkward; polishing Maya's line
into a clean scope statement would blow ep 7. Cam's pushback
(*but we looked everywhere we go, that's all we can do*) must read
as *honest and stuck*, not *stubborn or defensive* — he is not
fighting Maya, he is genuinely inside the data. Jules should be
quiet in this episode; her fear has thinned enough that she is not
driving the scene, and her quietness is part of what makes the
tempted-closure feel real (she would take the relief if it were
offered). The uncanny-not-horror-adjacent calibration still applies
but at its lowest volume yet: Jules's anxiety is almost dormant,
visible only in how still she is when Maya puts the pen down.
Priya's absence should feel like *a kid having a family thing* and
nothing more; the absence's structural weight is the reader's to
notice, not the characters' to dramatize.

## Authorial notes

Episode 3 is the alien-anxiety peak and the Cam-weakness peak in the
same scene. That concurrency is intentional. Jules's fear is loudest
here because the adult world has arrived in pieces through the
afternoon — a text here, an overheard *honey it's just squirrels*
there — and the *nobody is listening* feeling the design doc flags
is as emotionally present as it will ever be. At the same time Cam's
post is out and has done something, and the way Cam reads what it has
done is the episode's central epistemic failure: the replies are not
independent sources, the Frayser comment is not a verified sighting,
and the supportive ones get quoted twice while the skeptical ones get
waved away.

The `stalemate` shape here is not a dramatic beat. It is the shape
of a group that cannot, yet, resolve itself. Cam is pulling the
investigation toward *everywhere*, and doing so in a register that
sounds like progress. Maya is pulling it toward *genetics tomorrow*,
and doing so in a register that sounds like hesitation. Jules is
pulling it toward *this is real and nobody is listening*, and the
adult-dismissal texture is making that pull harder. None of the
three directions wins. The group's only decision is to meet again
tomorrow and try the thing Maya named. That tomorrow is episode 4.

On the two Cam targets: yes, two targets on one character in one
episode is heavier than the ep-1 or ep-2 allocation. The rationale
is that `overgeneralization` has a natural home in ep 3 per the arc
and nowhere else; `confirmation_bias` would otherwise only exist on
Anya, who is off-screen and cannot carry coverage. Splitting them
lets each cognitive pattern have a distinct behavioral trace (the
Frayser-everywhere leap for overgeneralization; the inside-the-
hypothesis audit for confirmation_bias) rather than collapsing both
into one target with two signals. The two targets also map to two
different facets — `sufficiency` for overgeneralization (quantity
problem), `source_diversity` for confirmation_bias (the replies are
not independent sources). The facets fit the cognitive patterns
cleanly; neither pairing is a stretch.

On deferring `source_diversity` vs. planting here: the design doc
lists Cam's carried facets as `source_credibility`, `source_diversity`,
and `sufficiency`. `source_credibility` lands ep 1 and ep 2; this
episode is the natural home for the other two. `source_diversity`
could have lived in ep 8 when he calls Anya himself, but ep-8 Cam is
starting to *do* the move right, not getting it wrong — so ep 8 is a
strength episode for source_diversity, not a weakness one. Ep 3 is
the right weakness episode.

On Maya's strength: `inferential_validity` and not `reasoning_completeness`.
The ep-1 strength was `reasoning_completeness` (naming the open
question). The ep-2 weakness was `reasoning_completeness` (softening
the half-objection). The ep-3 move is a *different* shape: Maya is
noticing that the chain being built has a missing node (*what else
could cause this*) and pointing at a framework that could fill it.
That is specifically an `inferential_validity` move — the chain should
have accounted for alternative causes, and Maya is the one who
notices. Her `reasoning_completeness` arc is about finishing chains;
her `inferential_validity` arc is about chains that *have the right
nodes*. Both need strength plantings. Splitting the ep-1 and ep-3
strengths across the two facets is cleaner for her arc than
repeating the same facet.

On Jules with no new target: Jules has carried weaknesses in ep 1 and
ep 2 and her cognitive patterns (`false_cause`, `black_and_white_thinking`)
are firmly planted. Adding a third target in ep 3 would be a third
consecutive weakness episode for her and would crowd Cam's double-
target planting. Her role in ep 3 is emotional weight: her fear peaks
here, audibly, in the *what if everyone's wrong* line, and the
adult-dismissal texture lands on her hardest. That weight is the
episode's engine; she does not need a designed reasoning move to
carry it. The design doc is explicit that Jules has no growth arc —
she is the engine — and ep 3 uses her engine-ness without re-planting
her weaknesses.

On Priya with no target, again: episode 4 is her weak-move episode.
Two consecutive non-target episodes for her (ep 2, ep 3) foreshadow
her ep-4 single-sentence weak move by establishing her as *usually
there, usually quiet, usually knowing what she's talking about* — so
that ep 4 can land the sentence *we've only ever seen them in the
Old Forest, so that's where they live* without the group pushing back
on it. Priya being quietly reliable across eps 2 and 3 is what
makes her ep-4 sentence read as reliable by default. This is
scaffolding that only becomes visible in retrospect.

## Why these targets

**sufficiency / Cam / overgeneralization.** The design doc names
overgeneralization as Cam's secondary weakness "surfacing cleanly in
episode 3" and specifies the move: a handful of replies, one
cross-town sighting, extended to *everywhere*. The facet is
`sufficiency` (the quantity/verification problem), not `source_diversity`
(which is the next target). The signal needs to show the *leap of
scale* concretely: one Frayser comment plus a handful of agreement
replies plus some mocking plus some curious, read on Cam's phone at
the picnic table, rolled up into a city-wide pattern. This is the
episode's central weak move and it has to be unmistakable — the
overgeneralization move should feel to the reader like the thing
6th-graders actually do when social media lines up behind a post they
made.

**source_diversity / Cam / confirmation_bias.** The story declares
confirmation_bias as a cognitive pattern, and the design doc assigns
it to Anya off-screen. Since Anya is never a lead, confirmation_bias
must also land on a lead somewhere or the coverage fails. Ep 3 Cam is
the natural home: he posted the hypothesis, the replies are responses
to *his* framing (not independent observations), and his reading of
the replies — quoting the supportive ones twice, waving away the
mocking, skipping the curious-skeptical — is the exact shape of
confirmation bias. Pairing the pattern with `source_diversity` as the
facet makes the move concrete: the failure is treating downstream
responses as independent sources. The pairing also feeds the ep-8
reversal, where Cam runs the same audit — this time, on his own post —
and sees that the sources do not diversify the claim.

**inferential_validity (strength) / Maya.** Maya's three carried
facets — `reasoning_completeness`, `internal_consistency`,
`inferential_validity` — each need at least one strength episode.
`reasoning_completeness` landed in ep 1 (naming the question).
`inferential_validity` lands here in ep 3 (naming the missing node,
pointing at genetics as a framework that could fill it).
`internal_consistency` lands in ep 5 (Cam reading an earlier sentence
back to her and her revising on the page). The proto-strength in ep
3 is deliberately soft — Maya does not lay out a chain, she gestures
at one — because her real inflection is ep 5. Ep 3 plants the seed by
having her do, for the first time, a *chain-aware* move: recognizing
that the chain being built needs a node it does not have. The move
is tentative enough that the stalemate shape holds.

## Calibration note for this episode

This is the alien-anxiety peak. Jules's *what if everyone's wrong*
line has to land as *isolated and tired*, not as *angry* or *certain*.
The adult-dismissal texture — the *lol, aliens* texts, the *honey
it's just squirrels* overheards — should register as *distracted and
dismissive*, not *hostile*. The story design doc's calibration
warning (uncanny, not horror-adjacent) applies at full force: Jules
is not afraid of being hurt, she is afraid of being *alone with the
question*. The antidote to that isolation is the three kids at the
table, and the closing beat — Priya saying something small and warm,
the notebook staying open with Maya's next-day addition below the
original question — should feel like *holding the question together*
rather than resolving it. The relief comes in later episodes. Ep 3
holds the weight.

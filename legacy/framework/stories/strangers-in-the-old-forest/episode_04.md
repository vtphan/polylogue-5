## Authorial notes

Episode 4 is the pivot from the Evidence stretch (1–3) into the Logic
stretch (4–5, 9, 10), and it is the quietest episode so far by
design. The alien-anxiety peaked in episode 3; the emotional
thermostat here drops because the four of them now have something
*to do* — remember what Ms. Reyes taught, apply it — and doing
something is itself a form of relief. The scene should feel, after
yesterday, like the group has a task and is trying, even though the
task does not finish. That trying-and-not-quite-finishing is the
self_correction shape's honest texture.

Two facet-level weaknesses land in this episode and neither has a
named cognitive pattern. This is deliberate and it matches the
design doc's treatment of both Maya and Priya: their weaknesses are
*habits*, not fallacy shapes. Maya's chain-building cannot externalize
yet (inferential_validity); Priya draws conclusions without tracking
the conditions under which she collected the observation
(condition_sensitivity). Both habits live below the surface of fallacy
vocabulary, and forcing a cognitive_pattern onto either would sharpen
the signal in the wrong direction — it would make the moves *read as*
fallacies (*confirmation bias! hasty generalization!*) when the whole
story rests on the idea that competent reasoning can fail in quieter
ways than fallacy-taxonomy captures. The signals live in the beats
and the transcript behavior, and the planning_agent should treat the
beats as the definitive source for behavioral trace here.

On Maya carrying both the weakness and the strength in the same
episode: this is deliberate and is what the design doc points at
("small self-correction — somebody, likely Maya, catches themselves
mid-sentence deferring to Anya's framing and stops"). The instinct
that catches the smuggled-in Anya premise (strength:
reasoning_completeness) is the *same* instinct that cannot yet build
the genetics chain (weakness: inferential_validity). The reader
should experience this as coherent, not contradictory: Maya's
instinct is fine, it has always been fine, what she has never built
is the muscle for *externalizing* the instinct into a chain on the
page. Catching a bad link is a smaller, more local job than building
good ones, and her instinct can still do the smaller job while
failing at the larger one. That is also why the episode works as a
runway into ep 5: the closing beat (*I have to write this out*) is
Maya naming what she has just realized she needs — and ep 5 is her
doing it.

On reasoning_completeness (not inferential_validity) as the strength
facet: the move is auditing whether a premise earns its place in the
chain, not auditing the link-quality between premises. *Did I do the
work to justify using Anya's framing here?* is a completeness
question — the chain was resting on a premise Maya had not examined.
Calling it inferential_validity would be technically possible but
would make the strength facet identical to the weakness facet in the
same episode, which is not what the doctrine needs. Keeping the two
facets distinct — weakness on inferential_validity, strength on
reasoning_completeness — reads cleaner for reviewers and also
reprises the ep-1 strength facet in a quieter form.

On Priya's move landing unpushed-on: this is the point. Priya is the
kid who usually knows what she is talking about, and her weakness is
not a visible fallacy but a habit of not tracking the conditions of
her data. For the weak move to land the way her arc needs, the group
has to *accept* it without audit. That acceptance is the episode's
structural work — the weakness is not that Priya said the sentence;
the weakness is that the sentence entered the record untested because
Priya said it. Episode 6 (where she is absent) and episode 7 (where
she names the sampling bias herself) are the payoff. In ep 4 the
sentence has to just *sit* on the notebook page, unaudited, so the
reader can feel its weight in retrospect.

On Cam having no target: his Evidence-weakness episodes (1, 2, 3) are
done. Ep 4 is his quiet — the space between planting and reversal.
The role he plays here is genuine-curiosity pusher: he is the one who
asks Maya *how though* when she lands on *it fits* without building
the chain. That question comes from the provenance habit his ep-2
grudging-TikTok-check seeded — *where does this come from?* is
transferring a little, quietly, to *how does this actually follow?*
It is not a target beat (he is not doing a designed weak or strong
move) but it is a *texture* beat, and the dialog writer should hear
his question as curious rather than critical.

On Jules in ep 4: she has no designed target and no growth arc. Her
engine-ness in this episode is inverted from ep 3 — yesterday she
was pulling toward aliens; today she is hoping the genetics chain
closes so her fear can dissolve. The chain not closing is why her
fear stays a little longer. Her quiet *so it could still be aliens
then, right?* near the end is not a re-assertion of the alien
hypothesis; it is the honest emotional state of a kid whose fear has
not yet been given a way out. The dialog writer should handle this
line carefully — tired, not combative, and not answered with yes or
no.

No social dynamics in this episode. The `group_pressure` texture from
ep 2 is dormant; Maya is not softening any objection out of care for
Jules because nothing objection-worthy has been asserted yet (Priya's
sentence is not the kind of thing Maya would push on yet, even absent
Jules's fragility). The `authority_deference` from ep 2 is being
*quietly audited* by Maya's self-correction — she catches herself
about to re-invoke Anya — but the audit is small and is a strength
beat, not a social-dynamic beat. No new social dynamic plants here,
which is correct: ep 4 is a Logic-primary episode with two habit-
level weaknesses and one quiet strength, and adding a social dynamic
would crowd the stage.

## Why these targets

**inferential_validity / Maya / (facet-level).** The design doc names
this as the episode where Maya's Logic weakness is "fully planted":
"In episode 4, trying to apply what the group remembers of Ms.
Reyes's Mendelian genetics unit, she cannot reconstruct the chain
from *recessive allele* to *observable red-eyed phenotype* even
though she can feel it should work." The behavioral trace is shape
(B): she lands conclusions with feeling (*it fits, I can feel it
fits*) and cannot externalize the steps when asked *how*. The gap is
between her instinct (which is working) and her articulation (which
is not there yet). This is a chain-having-the-right-structure
failure, not a chain-not-being-finished failure — hence
inferential_validity and not reasoning_completeness. Her ep-5
inflection (writing the chain out step by step for the first time in
her life) is specifically the reversal of this weakness, and the
closing beat of ep 4 (*I have to write this out*) is the runway into
that reversal.

**condition_sensitivity / Priya / (facet-level).** The design doc is
explicit that ep 4 is where Priya's weak move lands in its cleanest
single-sentence form: *we've only ever seen them in the Old Forest,
so that's where they live.* The sentence is true about the observation
(that is where they have *seen* them) and untrue about the conclusion
(the Old Forest is where they have *walked*, not necessarily where
the squirrels live). The move is a habit-level scope failure, not a
fallacy, and it has no named cognitive pattern for the same reason
Maya's weakness doesn't: the season's design trusts the facet label
and the beat to carry the signal. Episode 1 planted this weakness in
soft foreshadow form (*squirrels don't have red eyes anywhere I've
been*); episode 4 plants it in full form; episode 6 shows its cost
(the map of only the south side) with Priya absent; episode 7 is her
inflection. The sentence landing *unpushed-on* is the episode's
structural work and should be protected by the dialog writer.

**reasoning_completeness (strength) / Maya.** The self_correction
shape needs a designated strength beat and the design doc points at
"somebody (likely Maya) catches themselves mid-sentence deferring to
Anya's framing and stops." Assigning this to Maya — same carrier as
the weakness — is coherent with her characterization: her instinct
catches a smuggled-in premise even while her articulation fails at
building the chain around it. The facet is reasoning_completeness
rather than inferential_validity because the move is about *whether
the chain has earned its premises* (a completeness question) rather
than *whether the links between premises are sound* (an
inferential_validity question). Keeping the strength facet distinct
from the weakness facet in the same episode reads cleaner. The move
also reprises the ep-1 strength — Maya is the one who notices what
the chain is resting on — and the continuity is part of her arc.

## Calibration note for this episode

The emotional register drops here from ep 3's alien-anxiety peak.
Jules's fear has not dissolved — it is still present, quieter, and
her *could it still be aliens then?* line near the end is the honest
shape of unresolved anxiety, not re-assertion. The rest of the scene
is the group trying to *do something* with the mystery, which is
itself a form of relief after yesterday. The dialog writer should
treat the trying-and-failing of Maya's chain as warm, not discouraging
— it should read as *four friends remembering a class together and
finding out they can't quite build the chain yet*, not as *four kids
failing*. The self-correction beat is small and should not be staged
as a victory; Maya catches one thing, cleanly, and the scene moves
on. The closing — notebook open, tomorrow promised, Priya saying
something small and warm — should feel like the group *holding* the
investigation overnight, the way it held Jules's fear in episode 3.
The uncanny-not-horror-adjacent calibration still applies, but
quietly; Jules's anxiety is audible only to a careful reader in this
episode.

  report on the second call to Anya. Maya arrives a few minutes

  mutation at a pigmentation locus, long ago, recessive, carried
  silently by heterozygous grey squirrels. A founder population
  trapped in the Old Forest when the highways went in years ago,
  cut off from dilution. Quiet accumulation across generations.
  Two carriers mate, one in four offspring homozygous — a red-eyed
  white squirrel, because albinism hits eye pigmentation the same
  way it hits fur. Priya carries the ecological step of the chain:
  habitat-island, founder effect, why dilution didn't happen. Cam,

  Priya's mom, Ms. Reyes's notes, a second comparison park, the

  it closes on what they already have. Cam relaxes. Maya finishes.

  red-eyed squirrels in non-isolated parks, quiet accumulation
  over years. Cam notes plainly that nothing in the chain requires
  anything non-terrestrial. Jules watches this assembly happen
  around her — not without her, exactly, but not driven by her —
  and is quiet. The alien framing loses its last foothold in the
  scene not by being refuted but by being unneeded. The group
  agrees to bring the falsifiable reformulation to next meeting.

## Authorial notes

Episode 9 is Maya's Logic-lens inflection at full scale and the
season's chain-assembly payoff. It is the episode where the mundane
answer finishes being built. The emotional register carries over
from ep 8 — low-affect delivery of high-content — but the texture
is different: ep 8 was *processing information* (Cam reports, Jules
absorbs, Priya names, Maya softens); ep 9 is *building a structure*
(Maya speaks, Priya fits, Cam audits, Maya closes). The scene's
pace should feel like watching someone do competent work, not like
watching a revelation. The design doc's phrase for Maya here is
*faster and cleaner than the first, and nobody has to push her to
write it down* — this is the line the dialog writer should keep in
the frame.

On Cam's weakness landing on `sufficiency` rather than on a named
cognitive pattern: the overcorrection is a *habit-scope* problem,
not a *fallacy shape*. Cam's ep-8 provenance habit is real; what he
has not yet learned is when it stops applying. `sufficiency` is the
facet because the specific question he is confused about is *how
much evidence is enough* — and he is treating *enough to accept a
source* as equivalent to *enough to accept an implication chain*.
These are not the same question, and the scene's calibration
(Priya's *my mom would say the same thing Punnett would say*) names
the distinction. This leaves `sufficiency` properly unfinished as a
strength: the design doc commits sufficiency's positive landing to
ep 10, where Cam will apply the habit in its calibrated form. Ep 9
is the in-between state — skill present, scope not yet settled —
which is pedagogically the honest picture of how a new habit
actually develops.

On no named cognitive pattern or social dynamic for Cam's ep-9
weakness: his ep-1/2 `uncritical_acceptance` pattern and his ep-3
`overgeneralization` + `confirmation_bias` cluster are all
*under-auditing* shapes, and ep 9's overcorrection is the opposite
direction. None of the season's declared cognitive patterns fits
the shape — the declared patterns (`false_cause`,
`black_and_white_thinking`, `uncritical_acceptance`,
`overgeneralization`, `confirmation_bias`) are all about *accepting
too easily*, and Cam's ep-9 move is about *auditing past the
point of usefulness*. Leaving the pattern field empty is therefore
the honest annotation; the behavioral trace is captured in the
cognitive_signal field as stage direction rather than labeled
shape. The same is true of social dynamics: Cam's overcorrection is
not driven by group_pressure or authority_deference. It is
individual-skill clumsiness, and the scene's calibration move
treats it that way.

On Maya's strength being `inferential_validity` rather than
`reasoning_completeness`: these are distinct facets in the
framework and they do distinct work in Maya's arc. Ep 5 planted
reasoning_completeness — she finishes a chain that had one more
sentence in it that she had been avoiding. Ep 9 plants
inferential_validity — she builds a chain in which each step
actually *implies* the next, which is the validity question rather
than the closure question. The two facets are complementary and
Maya's arc runs through both, but conflating them would lose the
pedagogical point. Ep 9's chain is longer, structurally denser, and
integrates someone else's observation (Priya's habitat-island
sentence) as a load-bearing step — these are inferential-validity
markers specifically. A reader who has watched ep 5 and ep 9
should feel Maya moving from *I can finish a chain* to *I can
build a chain that closes on its own logic*.

On Priya's strength being `condition_sensitivity` rather than
`perspective_engagement`: Priya's two declared facets are taking
turns landing — `perspective_engagement` was the ep-7 and ep-8
beat (sampling-bias naming, friend-pressure naming); ep 9 returns
to `condition_sensitivity`, which was her ep-4 weak move and her
ep-7 *first-instance* strength (the highways cutting the park off
from the rest of Memphis). Ep 9 is where that first instance
becomes a load-bearing contribution to someone else's chain, which
is the move that earns the facet as settled rather than emerging.
This continues the arc pattern the design doc names — ep 4
weakness, ep 7 emergence, ep 9 settled — without any of the three
episodes duplicating the others. By ep 10 both Priya facets should
be available to her without prompting; ep 9 confirms
condition_sensitivity as the one that started the emergence.

On Jules not carrying a target in ep 9: the design doc's arc
summary for ep 9 is explicit — *Jules watches this assembly happen
without her and is quiet*. The same rationale that kept a target
off Jules in ep 8 applies: Jules is engine-not-growth, and putting
a weakness target on her in the chain-assembly payoff episode
would force a pattern-surface where the scene needs her quiet
acceptance. The fear she has carried since ep 2 is dissolving in
this episode not by being refuted but by being unneeded — that is
an emotional and structural move, not a cognitive-pattern move,
and staging it as a targeted beat would compete with what the
scene is doing. Her ep-10 noticing-without-pressing (Anya's
defensiveness on the third call) is where her last move lives; ep
9 is the quiet before that.

On the scene's staging as Wednesday-after-school: the story's
established rhythm is that the four meet at the picnic table on
Mondays and Wednesdays (and most weekends). Ep 8 is Monday; ep 9
is the next Wednesday — two days of gap for Maya to have been
thinking, which the scene needs to motivate her arriving with the
chain already half-built. The ep-9 Maya is not improvising in
real time; she has been working on this since Sunday's north-side
walk and Monday's Anya report, and she has arrived ready to lay it
out. That *arrived-ready* quality is part of what makes the scene
read as *competent work* rather than *revelation in the moment*.

On Cam's "nothing in this needs anything non-terrestrial" line
not being a formal strength target: this line is in the design
doc's arc summary and will appear in the transcript, but it is
not a pedagogical target in ep 9 — it is a summary move by Cam
that acknowledges what the chain has just done. The sentence is
the scene's resolution of the alien-framing question at the
logical level (*the chain does not require what the framing
predicted*), and it belongs to Cam because of his ep-3 stake in
the framing and his ep-8 audit of his own post. Targeting it
formally would crowd the episode past the design's committed
surface area; leaving it as staged texture inside his weakness-
target scene keeps the episode's pedagogical footprint clean at
one weakness and two strengths.

## Why these targets

**sufficiency / Cam.** The design doc states directly that Cam's
sufficiency lands as a strength in ep 10 and not in ep 8, and it
flags his ep-8 growth as *slightly overcorrecting — he wants the
group to audit everything and slows them down — because new skills
are clumsy.* Ep 9 is the natural landing for that overcorrection
as a visible weak move: the chain-building scene is exactly where
over-auditing can slow a process that is actually closing on its
own structure, and the group's calibration of Cam (rather than
correction of him) is the register the design doc has built for
the back half of the season. The facet is sufficiency rather than
any other Cam-carried facet because the specific confusion is
quantitative — *how much is enough* — and because leaving
sufficiency unfinished here is what lets ep 10 do its declared
work of landing it as strength.

**inferential_validity (strength) / Maya.** The design doc commits
Maya's second chain — *mutation, founder effect, heterozygous
carriers, and habitat isolation* — to ep 9 and specifies that it
be *faster and cleaner than the first* and that *she now writes by
default*. The facet is inferential_validity because the chain's
quality is that each step implies the next, not merely that each
step sits beside the next. Ep 5 planted reasoning_completeness
(closure), ep 9 plants inferential_validity (implication); both
facets are declared for Maya in the design doc and both are due a
strength landing by ep 10. Running inferential_validity in ep 9
leaves internal_consistency available for an ep-10 re-surface or
for retirement; reasoning_completeness remains planted from ep 5
and does not need to be retargeted here.

**condition_sensitivity (strength) / Priya.** The design doc
commits Priya's ep-7 seed sentence about the highways cutting the
park off to become a load-bearing step in the ep-9 chain. That is
the condition-sensitivity move specifically — the chain requires
that the allele frequency be preserved rather than diluted, and
preservation requires isolation, and Priya is the one who has the
isolation step available because she has been training the
scope-lens habit across eps 4, 6, and 7. Ep 9 is where that
habit becomes *useful to someone else's reasoning* rather than
*visible in her own observation*, which is the move that earns the
facet as a settled strength. Her second declared facet,
perspective_engagement, is held for ep 10 if needed, or retired
with the ep-7 and ep-8 landings intact.

## Calibration note for this episode

The register is *quiet competence*. Maya's chain-building should
not stage as triumphant — it is a kid doing the work she has been
preparing to do for five episodes, and the scene's dignity is in
its ordinariness. Priya's habitat-island step should not stage as
a teaching moment — she is offering the sentence that fits where
Maya has left a gap, same register as her ep-7 sampling-bias line.
Cam's audit demand should not stage as panic or as
obstructionism — it is earnest, the new skill looking for somewhere
to apply, and the scene's calibration should feel like friends
gently letting him know where this move belongs. Priya and Maya's
reframe of Cam is the ep-9 analogue of Priya's ep-8 naming of the
friend-pressure: quiet, short, same flat delivery. The dialog
writer should resist making anyone sound proud — not Maya about
the chain, not Priya about the ecological step, not Cam about his
new skill, not the group about having arrived somewhere. Jules's
quiet at the end of the chain should read as *acceptance without
struggle*, not as *defeat*. The alien framing is not being taken
away from her; it is becoming unnecessary, and the difference is
the whole point. The uncanny-not-horror calibration is fully
dormant by ep 9 — the fear has dissolved into relief across eps 5
through 8, and ep 9 is the structural confirmation that the
dissolution has a reason. The scene ends with four kids, a
closed chain, an open notebook, and the next move specified for
Thursday. Nothing is triumphant. Nothing needs to be.

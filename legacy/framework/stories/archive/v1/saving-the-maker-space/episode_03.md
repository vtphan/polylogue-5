## Authorial notes

This is the episode the season turns on. It has to do three things in roughly seven beats: stage the principal's reply in a way that lets a 6th grader follow the logic move (her reply is a model of how to engage with someone else's argument and still disagree with it), give Theo the moment of surprise, and end with the group in a posture they could not have been in at the start of episode 1.

The principal is the only adult in the season who gets dialog and her dialog matters a lot. She is not a villain. She is not even an obstacle in the conventional sense — she is what good reasoning *from the other side* looks like, and the kids' job for the rest of the season is to argue against her on terms she would accept. Her two lines in this episode ("which specific study supports them" and "tell me which number I should be wrong about") are doing the work of a textbook on how to engage with a flawed argument: she identifies the unsourced claim, she puts her own evidence on the table, and she invites the other side to point at where she is wrong instead of asserting she is. Students should leave the episode liking her, not just respecting her, and that is a deliberate creative call: the story is more honest and more interesting if the adversary is someone the kids could have been friends with.

The moment of surprise lives in beats 4 and 5. Theo's "she has a point" has to be quiet — not declarative — because the surprise is not that Theo disagrees with the group, it is that Theo is the one who refuses to let the group take the easy out (blame the principal). The whole setup since episode 1 has been pointing the audience toward expecting Theo to say "I told you so." When he says "I'm on the side where we don't get this wrong twice" instead, the audience should immediately recognize that this is what Theo has been about all along — his caution has always been about getting it right, not being right. That is in-character surprise: unexpected in the moment, recognizable in retrospect.

Mira's "whose side are you on?" is a small but load-bearing line. The story is about a group whose social cohesion has been letting them ship bad reasoning, and Mira reaching for "sides" instead of "evidence" in this beat is the moment where the social cohesion shows its cost openly for the first time. She is not the villain either — she is doing what has worked for her in the previous two episodes. The fact that it stops working here, on Theo of all people, is the inflection.

The closing beat (Theo asking for the petition draft, Mira hesitating then sliding it over) is the season's other turn. It is the first time anyone in the group has audited their own work in front of the others. Episode 4 picks up the day after, with Mira having spent the night thinking about that hesitation.

Sam is in the office and in the maker space conversation. She does not speak. Her silence in this episode is *loaded* differently than in episodes 1 and 2 — she is not staying silent because she has nothing to say, she is staying silent because the room is being rearranged around Theo's intervention and she is watching it happen. That sets up her own intervention in episode 4. The dialog should make sure the audience notices her noticing.

I considered putting `confirmation_bias` on Theo here (the inverse form the design doc commits him to). I decided to leave the inverse-bias in the prose, not in the targets list, because labeling Theo's behavior in this episode as a *weakness* contradicts the strength_prevails shape. The same prose phenomenon (Theo takes the principal seriously partly because she is contradicting the group) is doing the work of a strength here, and labeling it as a confirmation_bias weakness would confuse story_consistency_reviewer about which valence the episode's central moment has.

## Why these targets

**inferential_validity / Dev.** Dev has to carry the same failure into this episode, because the strength_prevails shape requires that *someone* keep defending the petition's framing after the principal has dismantled it — otherwise there is nothing for Theo's intervention to break. Dev is the right carrier because the design doc commits him to "decisive but reasoning-shallow" and his episode 2 framing is exactly what is being defended. The cognitive_signal is deliberately reactive (he is defending the framing because it was challenged, not because he is doubling down from conviction); this is the difference between Dev and Mira in this scene, and it matters for how their two failures land differently.

**sufficiency / Mira / confirmation_bias / group_pressure.** The lens is logic, not evidence, because Mira's failure here is in *processing* the principal's evidence rather than in failing to gather more. She has the spreadsheet. The failure is that she is treating the spreadsheet as something to find a hole in, not as something to think with. confirmation_bias under the logic lens is the right pattern label — she is not selecting evidence to fit her conclusion (she has been handed evidence that doesn't fit), she is selecting *interpretations* of that evidence to fit her conclusion. The social signal is the third iteration of her premature-consensus move, and the third iteration matters because Theo finally breaks it. The same move that worked in episodes 1 and 2 fails in episode 3, and the failure is what makes the strength land.

**source_credibility (strength) / Theo.** This is the season's payoff target for Theo and it has to live in this episode because the strength_prevails shape is structured around it. The surprise is the operator-only check; the strength is the validator-checked entry. They are the same beat seen from two angles. Note that the strength is *source_credibility* — not "good judgment" or "standing up to the group" — because Theo's caution across episodes 1 and 2 has been specifically about source quality (where Mira's NPR article came from, where Ren's three studies came from), and the principal's spreadsheet is what finally makes the source-quality question impossible to deflect. The audit Theo could not get the group to do, the principal does for them, and Theo's strength lands at the moment he refuses to let the group reject the audit just because the auditor is on the other side.

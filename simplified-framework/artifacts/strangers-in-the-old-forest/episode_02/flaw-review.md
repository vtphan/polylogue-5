## Warm-Up Candidates

Target: 2.

**Warm-up 1 — t05 (Jules).**
"Maya. She's a PhD student. In physics. At U of M. Marcus literally works with her. She knows what she's talking about, I promise."

- One speaker, one turn, one move.
- The flaw is the physics-credential-on-squirrel-biology mismatch, which is exactly the taxonomy's worked example for trusting_a_source_too_quickly.
- A 6th grader who has just been told "trusting a source too quickly means accepting someone as an expert without checking whether they're an expert in *this* thing" can point at "physics" and "squirrel" and see it.
- Clean distractor space: "not enough evidence" and "jumping to a conclusion" are both plausible but wrong — the turn is about *who* Anya is, not *how much* she said or *what follows from* it.

**Warm-up 2 — t11 (Cam).**
"Grad student. At U of M. Said our park. We were right on Saturday."

- Very short, very clean, very quotable.
- The whole turn is a stack of source fragments treated as a conclusion. A student can say "he's just listing who she is — he isn't giving a reason."
- Slightly easier than t05 for students who need a simpler entry point, because there is no mixed second flaw to disentangle.

## Level Candidates

Target: 3–5. Offering 4 strong options; the package builder can pick.

**Level candidate A — t08 (Cam).**
"A real scientist said biosignature. About our park. Bro, that has to be it then. That's not us guessing in the grass anymore, that's a PhD."

- Fits `jumping_to_a_conclusion` at `showcased`: one visible leap ("a PhD said a word" → "it has to be it"), one signal phrase ("has to be it"), single bridge missing.
- Also readable as trusting_a_source_too_quickly, which is useful — it lets the package builder pose a clean two-flaw distractor.
- Plain-language student gloss: "she just said a word — he's treating the word like proof."

**Level candidate B — t10 (Jules).**
"She said it's the kind of thing that shows up when something's happening in a habitat… so she confirmed it. That's her whole job, Priya."

- The sharpening move the plan explicitly asked for: Anya's "could be" becomes Jules's "confirmed."
- The setup is clean because Priya's t09 ("did she say it is or could be") is sitting one line above — the reader has the original wording in working memory.
- Dual-flaw turn, but in a good way: the jumping-to-conclusion move (could → is) is clearly separable from the trusting-a-source move ("that's her whole job").
- Plain-language student gloss: "Priya just asked if Anya said 'is' or 'could be,' and Jules answered as if Anya had said 'is.'"

**Level candidate C — t13 (Jules).**
"I don't know, from Rhodes, from a thing. It doesn't matter, she's a PhD student, she does this. Can we move on?"

- The refusal-to-audit move, unmistakable. Priya just asked a clean source question in t12; Jules waves it off.
- Great for the "they trusted that source too fast" student reaction — the speaker literally says "it doesn't matter."
- Strong distractor space vs. ignoring_another_perspective (which is a legitimate candidate but not yet introduced in the curriculum).

**Level candidate D — t14 (Maya).**
"I mean… if she said it, she said it. I'm not about to argue with somebody getting a PhD in it."

- The Maya-softens beat from the plan. Visible as an authority-deference move.
- Slightly harder than A–C because Maya's flaw is about not pursuing her own earlier question, which requires the student to hold t04 in memory.
- Good late-level candidate — rewards a student who has already caught the more obvious turns.

(t16 is viable as a fifth but duplicates t11's move in a summarized form. Recommend holding it in reserve unless a fifth is needed.)

## Why These Flaws Are Visible To 6th Graders

Each recommended turn supports a plain-language student reaction of the kind the framework prioritizes:

- **t05**: "They trusted that source too fast — physics isn't the same as squirrels." The mismatch is named on the page; no outside knowledge required.
- **t08**: "That does not prove it — one word from a scientist is not a conclusion." The leap is one step wide, and the signal phrase "has to be it" is right there.
- **t10**: "Priya just asked 'is' or 'could be,' and Jules answered like Anya said 'is.'" The student can quote Priya's correction and Jules's overwrite side by side.
- **t11**: "He's just saying who she is — that's not a reason." Three credential fragments stacked with no argument between them.
- **t13**: "She said it doesn't matter who Anya is — but that's the whole question." The refusal is in-line and self-naming.
- **t14**: "Maya stopped asking because Anya has a PhD. That's not a reason the squirrel part is right." Pairs cleanly with t04's interrupted question.

The episode also avoids what a 6th grader *cannot* see: there is no on-stage source audit, no expert vocabulary, no framework terminology, and no moment where catching the flaw requires the student to already know what a biosignature is. The word "biosignature" is introduced in-scene and treated as a borrowed term — the student does not need to evaluate the science, only the reasoning about who said it and how confidently.

## Weak Or Unclear Flaw Moments

None rise to the level of a required revision, but three items are worth the package builder's attention:

1. **t03's credential stack is slightly buried.** The line opens with "Anya. The grad student. Marcus knows her from Rhodes stuff." This is a nicely naturalistic mid-flight introduction, but the physics/biology mismatch is not yet on the page — that lands one turn later at t05. Recommend the package builder *not* quote t03 as a standalone flaw turn; use t05 instead. t03 is a context turn.

2. **Cam's t08 mixes two flaws.** The leap ("has to be it") and the source-trust ("that's a PhD") are both present. This is fine for a level question that distinguishes between the two flaws, but it would be a poor warm-up because the student has to pick one of two correct-sounding answers. Keep t08 as a level candidate, not a warm-up.

3. **The secondary `jumping_to_a_conclusion` showing is thinner than the primary.** The plan asked for two showcased leaps; the transcript delivers them (t08 Cam-leap, t10 Jules-sharpen), but they sit under the louder source-trust noise. A downstream package that wants a pure jumping-to-a-conclusion item should lean on t08; t10 is a mixed turn that works better as "which flaw is louder here?" This matches the plan's intent (secondary flaw, kept alive at lower level), so it is not a defect — just a note so the package builder does not try to force a pure-leap warm-up.

No turn triggers a taxonomy watchout. Jules and Cam sound enthusiastic and defensive, not stupid — the taxonomy's core warning for unmistakable source-trust ("stack the cues on the source, not on the speaker") is respected. Maya's soften in t14 is visible without being preachy. Priya's source questions in t09 and t12 give the reader a reference point without the scene doing the student's work for them.

One positive note worth flagging: the transcript correctly refuses to stage an on-stage source audit, in line with the plan's `avoid` block. Priya's questions land and are dropped; they do not resolve. That restraint is what makes the episode teachable rather than moralistic, and it sets up episode 7 cleanly.

## Warm-Up Candidates

- **Modeled (unmistakable) — t04.** Cam's TikTok stack. Four cues on the source
  in one breath; the line ends on "that basically proves something is
  happening," which is the sentence a student can underline as the flaw itself.
  The walk-through writes itself: count the cues, point at each one, notice
  that none of them tells us who made the video or whether they knew anything
  about squirrels.
- **Guided (showcased) — t11.** Cam's r/Memphis thread. Clean single-cue +
  signal-phrase shape that exactly matches the taxonomy's showcased sample
  line ("so that's not nothing, right?"). The prompt can ask what is weak
  about treating replies on an old post as confirmation; "anonymous comments"
  and "same kind of source" distractors are easy to write.

Both warm-up candidates are Cam turns. That is fine — Cam leads this episode
and the two moves are texturally distinct (TikTok vs. forum thread).

All 5 candidate turns are distinct (t04, t06, t09, t11, t14). The lesson
package will pass the pairwise-distinct-turn gate without adjustment.

## Why These Flaws Are Visible To 6th Graders

- **t04 (TikTok stack).** A sixth grader can count the cues: the account
  has no name, the video had a lot of views, the comments agreed. Each
  cue is about who saw it or how many people saw it, not about who
  knew what they were talking about. The student can say in their own
  words: "that doesn't tell us if the video was right."
- **t06 (university page).** The page is visibly credentialed but the
  student can be guided to notice that Cam never asks what Anya's
  actual research *is*, only that her page lists her as a PhD
  candidate. After brief instruction, a student can say: "the page
  makes her sound like the expert, but the kids didn't check whether
  it's the right kind of expert."
- **t09 (retelling).** The retelling uses "she literally said" as if
  that settles it. A student can be taught to ask: does the speaker
  actually know what the word she used means, and does what she said
  apply to squirrels? "She literally said it" is the cue.
- **t11 (forum thread).** The anonymous-reply move is the pattern
  students recognize fastest — "random people on the internet agreeing
  with other random people" maps cleanly to "that's not real evidence."
- **t14 (heightened defense).** The hardest of the five. The student
  has to catch that "grad student at U of M" sounds convincing but is
  credentialed-adjacent: physics ≠ biology, and nothing James said
  changes that. After the earlier moments, a trained student can
  quote the phrase "this is what she works on" and ask whether that
  phrase is backed up by anything specific.

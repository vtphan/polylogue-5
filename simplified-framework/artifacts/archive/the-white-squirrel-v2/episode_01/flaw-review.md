## 4. Why These Quiz Turns Work In The App

- **Visible in the reader.** Each flagged turn sits in a scene with its setup already on screen (the sighting in scene_01, Cam's page in scene_02, the "stars vs squirrels" clarification in scene_04). The app can attach a quiz under the turn without asking students to hold extra context in their heads.
- **One move per turn.** None of t23/t30/t39/t47 asks the student to untangle two flaws at once. Quiz prompts can be direct and short.
- **Promptable without restating.** The runtime convention is that the prompt does not quote or paraphrase the highlighted turn. All four candidates support a one-sentence direct question ("What is Cam doing with this source?" / "What is James doing here?") because the turn text does the heavy lifting visually.
- **Natural distractors available.** Each turn suggests 2–3 genuinely tempting wrong answers without needing invented content: for t23, "Cam is wrong about what he saw" vs "Cam is trusting the first result too quickly"; for t30, "James is making a joke" vs "James is piling up quick conclusions"; for t39, "Cam has no evidence at all" vs "Cam is using a phrase that only sounds relevant"; for t47, "Cam is ignoring Leela" vs "Cam is treating a space expert as a squirrel expert."
- **Feedback stays short.** Because each turn already contains the cue the student needs to point at, reveal copy can be direct ("Cam is letting 'first result' and a university logo do the work of checking whether Sophie's field fits the question") without having to compensate for a muddy moment.
- **Distinct scenes.** The lesson package can author three `levels[]` in three different scenes without renumbering or inventing a moment.

## 5. Why These Flaws Are Visible To 6th Graders

For each candidate, something concrete in the turn gives a beginner a place to point.

- **t23 (unmistakable).** A 6th grader can see Cam treating surface cues as the answer: "first thing that came up," the purple logo, a lab photo, a word they recognize as science ("space stuff"), and then "that basically settles it." The student can name the missing step in plain language: Cam never asked whether astrophysics is the kind of science that studies squirrels.
- **t30 (support unmistakable).** A 6th grader can count the "so"s. Four of them in one breath, ending in "aliens," gives the student a literal staircase to point at. Because each step almost sounds like it follows, the wrongness only shows up when the student reads the whole chain at once — which is exactly the teachable move.
- **t39 (showcased).** A 6th grader can see that the only "evidence" Cam offers is a phrase on a webpage plus "so that's not nothing, right?" The student can say out loud what the missing check is: does "unexplained observations" on Sophie's page mean unexplained squirrels, or something else?
- **t47 (heightened).** A 6th grader with brief instruction can catch that "she literally works on this kind of thing" is pretending to be a fit check without actually being one. Leela's flat line two turns later ("Astrophysics is stars. It's not squirrels.") is effectively a worked example of the exact skill the quiz is testing, which makes this band teachable even at the subtle level.

## 6. Weak Or Unclear Flaw Moments

Overall the transcript has no unusable candidates. The notes below are nudges, not blockers.

- **t47 placement softens the "trust without checking" frame slightly.** By the time Cam says "she literally works on this kind of thing," Leela has already pulled out her phone and said "Reading her page." No rewrite needed.
- **t28 ("It means something though") is an echo, not a quiz candidate.** Good; it reinforces the frame without competing with t23 or t39. Leaving it un-quizzed is correct.
- **t54 ("unexplained is unexplained though") is a faint echo of t39.** Because t47 has already been chosen as the heightened candidate, t54 should not also be promoted. If the lesson package ever needs a backup heightened candidate, t54 is usable but weaker than t47 (shorter, less phrase-doing-the-lifting) and should stay unused.
- **Leela's "Or albino" at t32** is the only on-stage alternative named before James' chain. It lives just upstream of t30, which actually strengthens James' flaw (he has an alternative in his ear and chains past it anyway). Worth noting for the package builder, but not a separate quiz candidate; this episode is not targeting `ignoring_another_perspective`.
- **No band is missing.** All three primary bands landed where the plan asked for them. There is no planning-vs-writing gap to flag.

Recommended revisions: none required. Optional micro-tweak a future pass could consider — but I am not recommending it for this episode — would be giving t47 one more beat of Cam's confidence before Leela's correction, to widen the room in which the heightened move sits. The current pacing is tight but teachable; do not touch it unless a later reviewer finds the moment actually fails in play.

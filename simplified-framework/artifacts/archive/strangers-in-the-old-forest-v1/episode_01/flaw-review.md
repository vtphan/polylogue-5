## Flaw moments found

### t03 — Jules
- **Flaw:** jumping_to_a_conclusion (unmistakable)
- **Why a 6th grader would see it:** The turn literally stacks four "so" clauses in one breath — "its eyes were red, **so** something is messing with it, **so** something in the park is doing it, **so** there's something here that's changing them" — and closes with the signal phrase "That basically proves it." A student can underline the so-chain and read the last sentence aloud as the flaw. It matches the taxonomy's `unmistakable` sample line almost beat-for-beat.
- **Warm-up candidate?** yes (strong)
- **Level candidate?** yes
- **Notes:** This is the cleanest, loudest instance in the transcript. The chain is fully self-contained inside one turn — no cross-turn setup needed, which is exactly what a warm-up wants.

### t05 — Jules
- **Flaw:** jumping_to_a_conclusion (showcased-leaning; doubles-down beat)
- **Why a 6th grader would see it:** After Priya's pushback in t04, Jules doesn't update — she repeats the move in a new shape: "If it looked like that, something **had to** make it look like that." The "had to" is the signal phrase the taxonomy flags. A student can see that Jules got pushed back on and didn't slow down.
- **Warm-up candidate?** no (needs t04 for context — "doubling down" only reads if you see the pushback)
- **Level candidate?** yes
- **Notes:** Not as loud as t03 on its own, but paired with t04 it's a teachable "she didn't listen and leaped again" moment. Good mid-level question territory.

### t06 — Cam
- **Flaw:** jumping_to_a_conclusion (unmistakable) + trace of trusting_a_source_too_quickly
- **Why a 6th grader would see it:** Cam moves from "I saw a TikTok" to "**so** stuff is definitely **happening** in this park." The base observation (a TikTok existed) is tiny; the conclusion (something is definitely happening) is huge, and the word "definitely" is the signal phrase. A 6th grader can say "a TikTok isn't enough to know that."
- **Warm-up candidate?** yes (second-best pick)
- **Level candidate?** yes
- **Notes:** The TikTok source cue is audible but the main teaching target is still the leap — "TikTok exists → something is definitely happening." The source-quality flavor is a minor secondary note, not a competing teachable flaw. That matches the plan's "avoid letting the leap land as source credulity." Keep.

### t09 — Priya
- **Flaw:** jumping_to_a_conclusion (unmistakable)
- **Why a 6th grader would see it:** Priya moves from "I've never once seen a white squirrel at Audubon" to "**so it has to be** something specific to this park." The signal phrase "has to be" and the jump from one person's memory at one park to a universal claim about "this park" is easy to paraphrase aloud. Students can say: "You only checked one other park — that's not enough to know."
- **Warm-up candidate?** yes (contender, but see notes)
- **Level candidate?** yes
- **Notes:** This is the trickiest turn to classify cleanly — it flirts with `not_enough_evidence` (one-park sample → universal claim). For episode 1 with `jumping_to_a_conclusion` as the loud flaw, this will read as a jump; the missing-evidence flavor is a shadow reading and that is fine. For warm-up purposes, t03 is cleaner because it is self-contained; this one relies on the "she only knows Audubon" background. Recommend using t09 as a **level** rather than warm-up.

### t10 — Jules
- **Flaw:** jumping_to_a_conclusion (unmistakable)
- **Why a 6th grader would see it:** Jules explicitly stacks three "so" clauses again ("it's not everywhere, it's here. **So** whatever is making them look like that is here, **so** something here is changing them, and nobody is even noticing"), and takes Priya's one-park observation as if it were settled evidence. This is the chain escalating into the "something nobody is noticing" frame the episode goal calls for. Maya even flags it in t11: "That's a lot of sos in one sentence." The meta-comment gives students a handle.
- **Warm-up candidate?** no (two-turn setup)
- **Level candidate?** yes (strong)
- **Notes:** Best level candidate in the back half. Maya's t11 beat is a gift for the question writer — the student can be asked what Maya is reacting to.

### t13 — Cam
- **Flaw:** group_consensus moment; jumping_to_a_conclusion (unmistakable, mild)
- **Why a 6th grader would see it:** Cam agrees ("Nah, she's right though") and adds the closing frame — "something is going on out here and grownups are just walking their dogs like it's whatever." The student can see the group has accepted the chain as settled even though nothing has actually been shown. This is the "consensus moment" the episode plan asks for.
- **Warm-up candidate?** no
- **Level candidate?** yes (optional)
- **Notes:** Softer than t03/t06/t10 because Cam is not stacking his own "sos"; he is ratifying. Good for a level question about group agreement, but if the level set already has 3–4 strong picks, this one can sit out.

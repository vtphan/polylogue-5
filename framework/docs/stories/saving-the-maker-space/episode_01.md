## Authorial notes

This is the establishing episode and it has to do four things at once: introduce three of the five characters in their stable voices, establish the social pattern (Mira's premature consensus → group nods → Theo's caution gets eaten) that will repeat for two more episodes before breaking, plant the early strength that collapses, and end on enough momentum that students want to read episode 2 even though nothing has technically gone wrong yet.

The "early strength collapse" shape is the hardest to make legible to a 6th grader, because the strength doesn't get a victory beat — it surfaces, gets ignored, and the episode keeps moving as if nothing happened. The way to land it is to put Theo's audit instinct in the middle of the episode, *not* at the end, so that by the time Mira's consensus statement closes the meeting the audience has already had two chances to see Theo almost do the right thing. The audience should leave the episode slightly uneasy without being able to point at exactly what went wrong. That unease is what the petition then ships on top of in episode 2.

Sam and Dev are not leads in this episode. Sam is in the room (she's at the table) but contributes nothing — that's the design-doc-established baseline her growth arc inflects out of in episode 4, so she has to be visibly silent here, not absent. Dev is at the table too but isn't a focus; his big move is waiting for episode 2. The episode mentions Dev once or twice in the dialog without making him a beat.

The cliffhanger is Theo at the closing beat — he is *almost* about to break the pattern. The bell rings. Episode 2 picks up after the petition has been drafted, deliberately *not* showing whether Theo asked Ren the question between episodes; the structural answer is that he didn't, but the audience finds that out by what happens in episode 2.

I considered putting `confirmation_bias` on Mira in this episode (she finds the article that confirms what she already believes about hands-on learning) but the cleaner read is that Mira's pattern is `false_certainty` (she treats how settled it feels as how settled it is), and Ren is the one whose source-gathering process is the confirmation_bias case. Two characters carrying two different patterns reads more clearly than one character carrying both.

The moment of surprise (rubric item 9) is not in this episode. It is in episode 3.

## Why these targets

**sufficiency / Mira / false_certainty / group_pressure.** This is the establishing instance of the pattern the season returns to. The design doc commits Mira to false_certainty as her stable cognitive pattern and to the premature-consensus move as her stable social contribution; episode 1 has to plant both and chain them together explicitly, because the same chain repeats in episodes 2, 3, and 4 with different consequences. Putting both signals on the same target in episode 1 makes the pattern visible from the start, which is necessary for the audience to recognize it as a pattern by episode 2 instead of as a one-time mistake.

**source_credibility / Ren / confirmation_bias.** Ren's design-doc identity is that she gathers genuinely (she actually goes looking for evidence) but cannot reliably distinguish source quality. Episode 1 has to establish the gather-vs-evaluate gap with a concrete instance specific enough that her growth in episode 3 reads as "she started doing the thing she didn't know to do," not as "the script needed her to grow." Three sources of obviously different provenance, presented as equivalent, is the clearest single beat for that. confirmation_bias is the right pattern label because the *mechanism* is selection by conclusion rather than by provenance — she didn't filter to support an existing belief, but the operative move is still that she let the conclusion drive what counted as evidence.

**source_credibility (strength) / Theo.** The strength has to be Theo and the facet has to be source_credibility because this is the season's payoff for Theo: his caution is the brake-that-isn't-a-brake, and the season is structured so the same instinct collapses in episode 1, surfaces unresolved in episode 2, and lands in episode 3. Episode 1 has to plant the instinct in a form the audience can recognize again three episodes later. The collapse is necessary, not incidental — if Theo's audit instinct lands in episode 1, the strength_prevails turn in episode 3 has nothing to prevail over.

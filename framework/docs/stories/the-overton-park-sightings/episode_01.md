## Authorial notes

This is the establishing episode and it has to do four things at once: introduce three of the five cast members in their stable voices (Jules's enthusiastic-chain speech, Cam's feed-shaped source-equivalence, Tye's quiet-notebook anchor move), establish the central mystery in a way that feels like a real observation Jules actually cares about (not a setup), plant the early strength that collapses (Tye's question sitting on the page), and end on enough unease that students want to read Episode 2 even though technically nothing has gone wrong yet.

The "early strength collapse" shape is the hardest to land legibly for a 6th grader because the strength doesn't get a victory beat — it surfaces, nobody picks it up, and the episode moves on. The way to make it land is exactly what *Saving the Maker Space* Ep1 did with Theo: put the strength move in the middle of the episode, not at the end, so that by the time the closing beat arrives the audience has already watched the group fail to engage with a move they should have engaged with. The audience should leave the episode slightly uneasy without being able to point at exactly what went wrong. That unease is what Episode 2 will pay off.

Maya and Priya are not leads in this episode. They are not mentioned in dialog either — the premise is that today is just these three after school, not all five. (Contrast with *Maker Space* Ep1 where Sam was in the room but silent; here Maya and Priya are genuinely not in the scene.) This is deliberate: keeping the cast tight for the establishing episode lets Jules's voice, Cam's voice, and Tye's voice each get enough room to be distinct before the larger group scenes in later episodes.

The cliffhanger is Tye closing the notebook with the question written and nothing underneath it. Episode 2 picks up after the group has decided to do a squirrel count — the decision happens off-screen between episodes — and the audience finds out that the question Tye wrote in Ep1 is the question Cam is now treating as "how many white squirrels are there," not "why." That misread is the engine of Ep2's count beat and Cam's first growth move.

Mixed-valence shape refinement. The design doc's arc summary tentatively characterized Episode 1 as "unresolved disagreement," but on writing the beats what actually happens is `early_strength_collapse`: Tye's stable-strength move (writing the question) surfaces and is not picked up by the group. There is no explicit disagreement in the scene — Jules and Cam are aligned with each other; Tye is not pushing back, he is anchoring — so "unresolved disagreement" is a thin fit. Early-strength-collapse is the honest shape and also improves the season's mixed-valence rotation (the design doc's arc summary will be updated to match). Note for the story consistency reviewer: the substantive commitment in the design doc (Tye writes the question, the group doesn't engage, the episode ends uneasy) is unchanged; only the label on the shape has shifted.

I considered putting `authority_deference` on Jules in Ep1 as a Marcus-deference move — she cites her brother repeatedly and accepts his framings without reconstructing them. The cleaner read is to hold `authority_deference` for Episode 3 where it lands on the zookeeper and pairs with `uncritical_acceptance` of her mother as "the same move wearing a different hat." Overloading Ep1 with both facets/patterns on Jules would crowd the dialog and blunt the sufficiency + false_cause pairing that does the work in this episode. Ep1 plants Jules's enthusiasm-for-Marcus in voice (she mentions him naturally), and Ep3 activates the authority-deference pattern proper.

Priya's one-line visible weakness move is in Episode 3, not here. The "kids know each other's neighborhoods from school talk" background is not needed for this episode because Priya is not present; it will matter in Episode 3 and beyond.

The moment of surprise (rubric item 9) is not in this episode. It is not yet pinned in the arc — probably Episode 6 (Priya's inflection) or Episode 7 (Cam pushing back on Jules's citation of the zookeeper). TBD in subsequent drafts.

## Why these targets

**sufficiency / Jules / false_cause.** The design doc commits Jules to `false_cause` as her primary cognitive pattern, `sufficiency` as a carried facet in Ep1, and "one sighting → whole hypothesis" as the episode's purest thin-reasoning instance. This target plants both in a single behavioral trace. The facet/pattern pairing is non-canonical per the framework's per-facet table (sufficiency's canonical biases are overgeneralization and false_certainty; false_cause's canonical facet is inferential_validity) — but the framework explicitly permits non-canonical pairings ("the framework does not treat the listed connections as privileged") and Jules's move is genuinely both thin AND a cause-leap. The two framings describe the same behavioral trace from two angles, which is exactly how the design doc frames the pairing — "two surfaces of the same episode-1 move." `interaction_note` on this target explains the non-canonical pairing so planning_agent and projection_reviewer do not flag it as a data-entry error.

**source_credibility / Cam / uncritical_acceptance.** Canonical pairing per the framework (source_credibility ← uncritical_acceptance). Design-doc-established: Cam's feed-shaped information environment treats sources as interchangeable, and the first instance of this is in Ep1 when he cites a TikTok in the same register as a news article. The specific TikTok he cites in Ep1 is the one he will audit in Ep2 as his first small growth beat, so this target is load-bearing for the Ep1→Ep2 transition. Signal is a clean behavioral trace (he pulls out his phone, shows the clip, neither names the source nor distinguishes its genre).

**reasoning_completeness (strength) / Tye.** Tye's stable strength is `reasoning_completeness` per the design doc — he keeps the chain unbroken because he writes things down and reads them back. Ep1 plants the instinct in the form the audience will recognize again in every subsequent episode: Tye opens a notebook, writes the question, reads it aloud. The early-strength-collapse shape is necessary, not incidental — if Tye's question lands in Ep1 and redirects the conversation, the season has nowhere to go, because the whole investigation is supposed to unfold as the group gradually earning its way back to the question Tye already wrote. The collapse is also what sets up Ep5's "tempted to close the notebook and resists" moment: the notebook Tye rereads in Ep5 is *this* notebook, and the question on the first page is *this* question.

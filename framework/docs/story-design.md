# Story Design

Operator guidance for designing a Polylogue story under the **episodes-first authoring model**. Written for the person authoring a story design doc and per-episode drafts, and the curriculum coordinator reading over their shoulder.

For the pipeline architecture and field schemas, see `pipeline-architecture.md`. For the end-to-end runbook, see `operator-manual.md`.

---

## What a Polylogue story is

**Story.** A self-contained multi-episode narrative with a fixed cast, an arc across episodes, and a declared coverage contract. Captured in two kinds of artifact, both authored as prose:

- **The story design doc** at `framework/stories/{story_id}.md`. Markdown with YAML frontmatter (`story_id`, `title`, `coverage_mode`, `declared_facets`, `declared_cognitive_patterns`, `declared_social_dynamics`, `episode_count`) plus a prose body (premise, setting, cast, arc summary, stakes, pedagogical commitments). The frontmatter is the machine-readable contract; the prose body is the source of truth for character identity.
- **The per-episode drafts** at `framework/stories/{story_id}/episode_{NN}.md`, one per episode. YAML frontmatter (the operator's authoring artifact — see the template in §10) plus prose body (beats, authorial notes, why-these-targets).

**Episode.** One discussion within a story. Generated by `/create_episode` → `/create_transcript` → `/build_assistive_package` and stored at `artifacts/{story_id}/episodes/episode_{NN}/`.

**Passage.** A subdivision within an episode, covering a subset of turns (`turn_range`). An episode typically contains 2–4 passages. Most pipeline output lands at passage or turn level.

**Turn.** One speaker's contribution in the transcript. The per-turn intervention dictionary and orientation probes are keyed to turns.

**Cast.** The recurring characters defined in the story design doc's prose. No separate cast schema; the design doc IS the cast definition.

**The design inversion.** The legacy system asked: pick facets → invent personas → write dialog. The story system inverts this: design a cast you find interesting → plan an arc that surfaces their tendencies in different episodes → check that the cast collectively covers what the framework requires. The cast makes the stories interesting; the cast's engineered distribution of weaknesses and strengths guarantees coverage. These two requirements are no longer in tension.

## The cast: six design rules

These rules are load-bearing — `story_consistency_reviewer` checks them. They also encode design wisdom you should believe in, not merely satisfy.

**1. The cast collectively carries the declared coverage.** Not in every episode — somewhere in the season. What is forbidden is declaring coverage of something the cast cannot plausibly carry.

**2. No character is an embodied fallacy.** Each character carries 2–3 cognitive tendencies and contributes to one social dynamic. Tendencies surface or stay dormant depending on the situation. A character whose only function is demonstrating one bias is decoration, not a person.

**3. Each character has at least one stable strength they reliably bring.** Strengths are how the cast teaches that good reasoning is a practice everyone is capable of.

**4. At most two characters in the cast have visible growth arcs.** Constancy is the default; growth is the exception. Growth is dramatically expensive and students need stable reference points.

**5. The cast collectively models the three lens dispositions.** Logic-leaning, Evidence-leaning, Scope-leaning — embodied, never named in dialog.

**6. Cast size is bounded: 4–6 characters total, 2–3 leads per episode.** If a character can be removed without breaking coverage, remove them.

**Strength-carrier rotation.** `validate_story.py` flags if any one character carries more than half the strengths (or weaknesses) in a story.

### Cast prose and growth

Cast tendencies live in **prose** in the story design doc. Each character gets a section describing how they reason: voice, cognitive moves, social dynamic, and (if they have a growth arc) the inflection that shifts how they reason mid-story.

Growth arcs are expressed in two places:
- In the design doc's prose, by describing the character before and after the inflection.
- In the per-episode drafts, by writing pre-growth signals before the inflection and post-growth signals after.

## The coverage contract

**Two modes.** Declared in the design doc's YAML frontmatter.

- **Full coverage** — all 10 facets, all 8 cognitive patterns, all 3 social dynamics. Default ambition for 6+ episodes. The validator fails the story if any is uncovered.
- **Focused coverage** — a declared subset. For shorter stories. Hard floor: ≥3 facets, ≥1 cognitive pattern, ≥1 social dynamic.

**What `validate_story.py` checks** across all per-episode draft frontmatter:

- *Coverage closure.* Every declared facet must appear as both a weakness and a strength somewhere.
- *Lens distribution.* Each lens primary in ≥1 episode; no lens primary in >half.
- *Mixed-valence rotation.* No single shape in >half of episodes.
- *Strength/weakness rotation.* No character carries >half of either.
- *Hedged-annotation rule.* Every declared pattern/dynamic must have at least one unhedged (single-label) annotation somewhere in the story. Runs only after Phase 7 produces analyses.

**On hedging.** Hedged (multi-label) annotations are honest calibrations, encouraged when evidence underdetermines. They do **not** satisfy coverage. The correct response to persistent hedging is to revise the draft so evidence becomes unambiguous — never to pressure the evaluator to commit harder.

## Evidence grounding

Every cognitive pattern and social dynamic must be visible in the transcript — not projected onto it.

**Per target in the episode draft frontmatter:**
- `cognitive_signal` — one sentence describing the behavioral trace of the cognitive pattern. Required iff `cognitive_pattern` is non-null.
- `social_signal` — one sentence describing the move/response turn-pair. Must reference at least two turns. Required iff `social_dynamic` is non-null.

**Social dynamics structurally require a turn-pair** (a move and a response). The planning agent translates the move/response pair into two adjacent `accomplishes` entries in `episode.yaml`'s turn outline.

**`evidence_basis`** is required in every analysis annotation, pointing to specific behavior that supports the pattern/dynamic assignment.

## The information barrier

Two facts shape what stories you can write:

- **The dialog writer never sees the design doc, the per-episode draft, or `episode.yaml`.** It receives a barrier-safe projection (`episode_writer_input.yaml`) that contains only persona voices, knowledge, weaknesses-as-prose, strengths-as-prose, and a turn outline whose `accomplishes` entries describe narrative beats without naming any framework category.
- **This means your `cognitive_signal` and `social_signal` prose must be stage directions, not analysis.** "Maya overstates how settled the question is from one remembered article" projects cleanly. "Maya exhibits false_certainty driven by overgeneralization" would break the barrier.

Two enforcement mechanisms catch leakage: a literal scan in `validate_schema.py` and a `projection_reviewer` agent that checks for paraphrased leakage. Neither alone is sufficient; both run on every projection.

**Practical upshot:** write per-target signals in the voice of *what the character does and how it sounds*, never in the voice of *what the framework categorizes it as*.

## What `story_consistency_reviewer` checks

Prose-on-prose review of the story design doc and every per-episode draft. Invoked routinely: after each new draft, as a final Phase 6 pass, and after any Phase 7 draft revision.

**Rubric items scored:**

1. Are the stakes concrete and personal to the cast?
2. Is the cast small enough (4–6) and distinct enough?
3. Does the arc have momentum across episodes?
4. Does the coverage contract close? *(also mechanical)*
5. Is mixed-valence varied? *(also mechanical)*
6. Does the ending earn its lack of tidy resolution?
7. Does any character feel like an embodied fallacy?
8. Would a 6th grader want to know what happens next?

A story that fails 1, 4, or 7 is not ready.

**Item 9 is human-only.** "Is there at least one moment of genuine surprise — a character doing something unexpected but in-character?" You have to put this in by hand.

## Designing for the pipeline

The pipeline produces four authored files per episode (see `pipeline-architecture.md` §2). Rich story → rich analyst output → rich downstream, for free. **You cannot compensate for a thin story by running the diagnostic agent harder.**

### What the analyst needs

**Cite-able evidence turns.** Every facet must be exhibited in visible behavior at a specific turn — not implied, not a vibe. Design beats at the sentence level. Authoring test: *can you point at the specific sentence and write a one-line description of what they're doing wrong (or right)?*

**A discrimination surface.** Passages need *near-misses* — moments where a student could plausibly flag the wrong facet. At least one `facets_absent_but_tempting` entry per passage. Authoring test: *if a 6th grader flagged the wrong facet, which one would they most plausibly flag?*

**Afforded-missing cells.** `lens_visibility` has two orthogonal axes: `engagement` (how much characters used the lens) and `affordance` (how much the topic gives the lens to work with). The most pedagogically valuable combination is `(engagement: none, affordance: rich)` — the topic richly supports the lens but no character engages it. This becomes a first-class intervention cell at maximum urgency. Design passages where at least one lens has rich affordance but zero engagement.

**Perspective transitions.** Every passage must afford at least one lens-to-lens transition. Narrowly single-lens passages are structurally impoverished.

**Turn-specific counterfactuals.** Per facet present, a one-sentence "what would fix this" that cites the turn and names a specific action. Generic prescriptions ("be more careful") are rejected. These feed the worked-example ladder rungs — thin counterfactuals → thin worked examples.

**Causal layer interactions.** For 8 of 10 facets (all except `relevance` and `inferential_validity`), a social dynamic must be in play. The beat must have a genuine social reason the mistake stuck. Authoring test: *"The [social dynamic] makes the [cognitive pattern] [harder to catch / unchallengeable]."*

**Cross-episode echoes.** Facets from early episodes should resurface in later ones. `connects_to.echoes[]` feeds the `prior_exposure` mechanism. A story where every episode is self-contained doesn't feed faded assistance.

### What the diagnostic agent needs

**Multi-interpretable load-bearing turns.** The orientation probe needs 2–3 plausible options per turn, drawn from present facets, afforded-missing facets, and tempting-absent facets. A turn supporting only one reading produces a weak probe. This doesn't mean every turn should be load-bearing — but the ones that are should carry multiple layers.

**Ladder rungs are largely lifted.** Three of six rung types are deterministically lifted from ground truth by the merge script: `worked_example` ← `counterfactuals[]`, `lens_switch` ← `perspective_transitions[]`, `redirect` ← `facets_absent_but_tempting[].why_wrong`. Rich story → rich analyst output → rich ladders, for free.

**Explanation depth is opt-in.** `has_explanation_depth: true` is set only where "why" is pedagogically load-bearing — typically cells with non-trivial `interaction` values or `(engagement: none, affordance: rich)`. Aim for 2–4 depth cells per episode, not every cell.

### What the prose agent needs

**A landable one-paragraph hook per episode.** The `episode_opening` sets the scene in one paragraph. If you need two paragraphs or prior-episode citations, the episode is under-crystallized.

**Actionable starter stems per passage per lens.** `entry_prompts[]` scaffold writing production: "I noticed that in turn ___, ___ assumes ___." Each (passage, lens) pair with nonzero affordance must support this.

**A crystallized closing question.** `consensus_check[]` asks what the group decided or failed to decide. If you can't name the question in one sentence, the episode is unfocused.

### What the discussion agent needs

**Lens-crossable moves.** The lens-refraction axis refracts an observation through a different lens. Beats that only make sense through one lens starve this axis. Tied to the perspective-transitions requirement.

**Distinct character voices.** The persona-projection axis asks "what would character X ask?" This is mechanically load-bearing — if every character would give the same answer, the axis is flat.

**Steelman-able positions.** The stance-inversion axis asks "defend the opposite." Beats where a character is unambiguously, indefensibly wrong can't be inverted. Characters should be wrong *because they believed something defensible that didn't apply here*.

**Continuation chains.** `continuation_of` indexes group cues to individual-phase probe taps. Beats where a private insight naturally continues into a group discussion produce rich handoffs. Design the individual→group transition so private insights have somewhere to go.

## Capability flags

Declared in the story design doc frontmatter. Default to all off; turn on only what the story needs.

- `pedagogical_register: unfinished_not_wrong | neutral` (default: `neutral`) — binary pedagogical framing, not genre. Genre is set in the story's prose.
- `uses_character_growth: true | false` (default: `false`) — enables `growth_beats` and `character_arc_position`. Requires growth arcs per cast rule 4.
- `declares_calibration_warnings: true | false` (default: `false`) — merge script lifts author-written warnings from the design doc.
- `uses_stance_positions: true | false` (default: `false`) — enables `stance_positions[]`.
- `supports_jigsaw: true | false` (default: `false`) — enables `jigsaw_fragments[]`.

## Re-planning loop

Episode-level reviewer failures bubble up to the story level when persistent:

1. `transcript_reviewer` flags an ISSUE on signal landing → `/create_transcript` re-invokes `dialog_writer` with feedback (one retry).
2. If the signal fails a second time, the episode is a structural problem. Re-run `validate_story.py`; the audit will show the affected pattern/dynamic as missing unhedged coverage.
3. The operator revises the per-episode draft's signals/carrier, or the story design doc if drift is character-level, and re-runs from the affected episode forward.

The story is not allowed to ship with a broken coverage contract. Never pressure the evaluator to satisfy coverage.

## What makes a story excellent

Above the structural floor:

- **Stakes that 11–13 year olds will actually care about.** Concrete, local, and personal.
- **A premise specific enough to feel real but general enough to travel.** Generic place names and constructed school cultures.
- **Cast voices distinct enough that students can predict them.** By episode 3, students should attribute unattributed lines better than chance.
- **An arc with momentum.** Each episode ends with a reason to come back.
- **At least one moment of genuine surprise** — a character doing something unexpected but in-character.
- **An ending that resists tidy resolution.** Mixed-valence at the story level.

## Per-episode draft template

The per-episode draft lives at `framework/stories/{story_id}/episode_{NN}.md`. It is what `/create_episode` reads.

### Frontmatter schema

```yaml
---
story_id: kebab-case-identifier
episode_number: 1
title: "Human-readable episode title"
premise: >
  One paragraph in narrative terms — situation, stakes. No framework vocabulary.
lead_characters: [Maya, Jordan, Sam]
primary_lens: logic | evidence | scope
mixed_valence_shape: early_strength_collapse | strength_prevails | stalemate | self_correction | unresolved_disagreement

previously: >
  Narrative recap of earlier episodes. No framework terms. Empty string for episode 1.

targets:
  - facet: facet_id
    lens: logic | evidence | scope
    carrier: Maya
    cognitive_pattern: cognitive_pattern_id    # null if none
    social_dynamic: social_dynamic_id         # null if none
    cognitive_signal: >
      Required iff cognitive_pattern is non-null. One concrete sentence
      describing the behavioral trace. Stage direction, not analysis.
    social_signal: >
      Required iff social_dynamic is non-null. One sentence describing
      the move/response turn-pair. Must reference at least two turns.
    interaction_note: >
      Optional. How cognitive and social signals interact.

strengths:
  - facet: facet_id
    carrier: Jordan
    note: >
      Optional. How the strength shows up.

beats:
  - "One sentence per beat. Five to eight beats per episode is typical."
---
```

### Prose body

Below the closing `---`, free-form Markdown. Two conventional sections:

- **Authorial notes** — what this episode is for in the arc, what you want students to notice, cliffhangers, callbacks.
- **Why these targets** — one paragraph per target explaining why this facet/pattern/dynamic landed on this carrier in this episode.

`story_consistency_reviewer` reads the whole prose body; `planning_agent` reads only the frontmatter.

### What the frontmatter must satisfy

1. Every field present and well-typed; `cognitive_signal` non-empty iff `cognitive_pattern` is non-null; same for `social_signal`. *(validate_schema.py)*
2. Every ID resolves to `framework/reference/`. *(validate_schema.py)*
3. Every carrier named in `lead_characters` and described in the story design doc. *(story_consistency_reviewer)*
4. Cross-episode rules (lens distribution, rotation, coverage). *(validate_story.py)*
5. Character behavior consistent with the design doc. *(story_consistency_reviewer)*

---

## The two artifacts and how they relate

The **story design doc** is the vision and source of truth for character identity. The **per-episode drafts** are the source of truth for what happens.

The two should not drift apart. `story_consistency_reviewer` reads both and verifies they agree. Expect at least one revision pass on the design doc as a result of writing the drafts.

## Suggested starting frames

Three contrasting frames for the catalog:

**Frame A — Saving the Maker Space** (civic, internal stakes). 6th graders save their school's maker space. Stakes concrete and local. Real disagreement about means. Partial-win ending. *(Shipped as v1 pilot; frozen in archive.)*

**Frame B — The Beat** (journalism, evaluating others' reasoning). Student newspaper/podcast. Each episode covers one story. Gets students out of "planning our own project."

**Frame C — The Capsule** (mystery, evaluating reasoning artifacts). Class opens a 50-year-old time capsule. Each episode discusses one artifact. Highest format variety.

**V1 storylines.** Creative briefs extracted from frozen v1 stories live at `framework/stories/v1-storylines/` — premise and arc only, no targets or signals. These can serve as starting points for new v2 stories with new story IDs.

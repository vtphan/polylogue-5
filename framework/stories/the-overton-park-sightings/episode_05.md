## Authorial notes

Stalemate is the hardest mixed-valence shape in the season because it refuses the two easy satisfactions: no character decisively wins and no character decisively loses. The episode has to end with the answer visible on the table, the notebook kept open, and the reason for keeping it open unnamed by anyone in the scene. That asks more of the reader than any previous episode has asked — the reader has to see the scope gap that the three present kids cannot see, and the reader has to come away with a specific piece of information (all four sightings are on the south-side loop) that none of the three characters has articulated. Every other authorial decision in this episode is in service of that reader-demand, and the episode will fail if any character in the scene names the scope problem out loud.

**The map as a physical object in a dialog-only transcript.** The biggest craft problem in Ep5 is that the map is the whole episode and the transcript has no way to show it. The solution, already embedded in the beats: the map surfaces entirely through the kids reading coordinates and locations aloud. Tye reads dates; Cam reads locations in plain language ("by the shelter," "playground side," "Old Forest edge south," "loop trail past the shelter"); Maya places dots on the map. Every location the kids speak aloud is on the south side of the park because every sighting in the group's records is on the south side of the park. The reader builds a mental map out of the location-phrases, and the reader's mental map turns out to be unanimous: south side, south side, south side, south side. The dialog writer must resist the temptation to give any kid a line like "wait, these are all in the same area" that is explicit about the arc-shape of the clustering. Cam's "they're basically right next to each other" in beat 3 is the maximum allowable geographic-notice in the episode, and it stays at the "close together" level of observation — not "on the south side specifically." Even Maya's "wait, that IS a pattern" in beat 4 is about the clustering, not about which half of the park the clustering is on.

**How to keep the three present kids plausibly unable to name the scope problem.** This is the other central craft problem. Each of the three has a specific reason they cannot name it:

- *Maya* is in the afterglow of her Ep4 chain and her attention is on the biology the chain describes, not on the geography the data came from. Her new Logic strength is narrow — she has learned to build and check inference chains — and it does not extend to interrogating the scope of data her chain applies to. That gap between "the chain is right about what it explains" and "I haven't asked what the chain explains" is the exact gap that Priya's Ep6 arc will close. Maya cannot see it because she has never been taught to see it, and the story refuses to have her suddenly acquire the machinery off-screen.
- *Cam* is the Evidence grower and his seed-strength is source-auditing (provenance, credibility, who made this). In Ep5 he actually does notice, in the fold-motion in beat 9, that the dots are all on one arc of the map — but noticing and naming are different things, and Cam does not yet have the concept that would let him translate "these are all in the same area" into "we have only sampled one area." His Ep6 unprompted provenance beat is about information sources (who made this claim?), not about data sources (what scope did our measurements cover?). The two are adjacent but distinct, and Cam's growth has not crossed the boundary yet. Dialog writer: Cam can physically handle the map in a way that makes the arc visible, but he must not verbalize what his hands are seeing. The seeing-without-naming is a real move for Cam and the audience should feel it happening even without anyone pointing at it.
- *Tye* is the notebook-keeper and his stability-move is to reread the original question when something feels done too early. That move, in this episode, gets him as far as "the question is bigger than the answer." It does not get him to "the question is bigger because the answer only covers the south-side loop." Tye does not have the word "sampling" or the word "scope" in his vocabulary, and he will not acquire them in this episode. He holds the notebook open on instinct and cannot defend the instinct when Cam asks him why. That is his stability-tested-and-holds beat in its honest form: stability in a sixth grader looks like refusing to close a notebook you can't yet defend keeping open.

The person who would have named it is Priya, and the whole premise of the stalemate is her absence.

**Tye's tested-and-holds beat.** The design doc commits Tye to a stability test in Ep5 — he is tempted to close the notebook and resists by rereading the original question from Ep1 out loud. The beats render this as a two-reading sequence in beat 7, and the dialog writer has to hit the second reading in particular: the first reading is Tye doing the move, the second is the move producing something (the shape of a gap). The half-beat of silence around the second reading is non-negotiable — if Cam or Maya speaks during or immediately after the second reading, Tye's move has no room to land, and the whole tested-and-holds beat collapses into just "Tye read something and nothing happened." The silence is how the reader hears Tye notice.

There is a real risk that Tye's beat will read as a breakthrough rather than as stability, because any move that produces new attention in a 6th-grade character tends to scan as growth. The guard against this is that Tye's noticing is shaped, not named: he does not come away with a concept or a next action, only with an unformed shape of gap and an insistence that they keep going. Dialog writer should render his "I don't know" lines in beat 8 with full honesty — he really does not know what he just noticed, and pretending otherwise (giving him a line like "I think we might be missing something") would turn stability into growth and break the character. Tye's line should be exactly the shape of "the question is bigger than — I don't know" with a trail-off, not the shape of "I think we need to look more places."

**Maya's quiet overreach.** Maya's `condition_sensitivity` / `false_certainty` target is the subtlest weakness in the episode and the one most at risk of being dialog-writer'd away by an instinct to protect her after her Ep4 win. The note to preserve is: Maya's new Logic strength is real and her Ep5 miss does not erase it. She is allowed to be satisfied with her chain. She is allowed to be briefly annoyed when Tye holds the notebook open. Both of those register the growth as earned (she now has a formalism and is defending it) while also registering the new gap (she has not learned to audit scope). If the dialog writer tries to give her a line like "well, my chain only covers what we've seen so far" they will erase the target, because then Maya IS auditing scope, and the weakness does not land. The target is specifically that she does not think to ask. Do not have her almost-think-to-ask and then stop. Have her not think to ask.

**Jules's absence.** Jules is absent with one mention (at Marcus's apartment at Rhodes). The absence matters: Jules's enthusiasm-engine register, if present at the edge-of-closure beat, would push decisively toward declaring victory, and the stalemate shape needs the temptation to come from internal satisfaction rather than from external push. With Jules in the room, the near-closure beat would read as Jules-bullying-the-group-into-done, and Tye's reopening move would read as resisting Jules rather than as stability. Isolating the temptation inside Tye's own chest is what makes the resistance characterologically his.

**Cam's return after two absences.** Cam has been absent Ep3 and Ep4 and returns in Ep5. The absence is not remarked on — he just shows up with a folded map from the library printer. That matches the design-doc treatment of Cam's off-screen beats (his mom's push in Ep2 happened at breakfast; his map-printing in Ep5 happens at lunch). Cam's Ep5 role is to handle the map physically, read the locations, and have a fold-motion in beat 9 where his hands see the arc his words cannot name. He does not carry a target and does not carry a growth beat in Ep5. His Ep6 unprompted provenance beat is two days away and needs room; loading Ep5 with a Cam target would crowd the stalemate shape and cost Cam's Ep6 beat its air.

**Priya's absence, second appearance.** Priya was absent in Ep4 by one line; she is absent in Ep5 by one line, and her absence is the structural load of the episode. The reader does not need to be told that Priya would have caught this — the reader needs to notice, after Ep5 closes, that the one person in the cast who reasons fluently about whose-Memphis-is-missing is the one person who was not at the table. If the dialog writer name-checks Priya as "well Priya would probably know" in any character's voice, the stalemate's rhetorical point collapses: the whole thesis of the episode is that the group cannot name what they are missing, and having a character name Priya as the thing they are missing is itself a naming of the gap. Do not name Priya after the cold-open mention.

**Moment of surprise.** Still not in this episode. Ep5 is in the running less than Ep6 and Ep7 for the formal moment-of-surprise slot — the stalemate shape specifically rules out surprise (stalemate is the absence of decisive movement, and surprise requires decisive movement). The candidates remain Ep6 (Priya's inflection when she walks the north side and names the sampling bias out loud) and Ep7 (Cam pushing back on Jules's citation of the zookeeper). Ep5's job is to load the bow that one of those two episodes will release.

**Mixed-valence shape.** `stalemate` is the Ep5 commitment from the updated arc summary and the episode is a clean fit. No character wins; no character loses; the answer is on the table; nobody names the gap; the notebook stays open; the three agree to meet again. That is the shape. Dialog writer must resist the urge to give the episode a resolution of any kind — not even a small one. A small resolution would break the shape. The episode ending with the word "south" unspoken, the four dots clustered on one side of a map nobody articulates the geography of, and a notebook held open without a defended reason is *the correct closing register*. It will feel slightly unsatisfying in the transcript. That unsatisfaction is the move.

**Turn budget.** 12 to 14 turns. The episode is less dense than Ep3 (three targets) but denser than Ep4 (one growth move) because the plotting rhythm in beats 2–4 needs enough turns to build the map in the reader's head — four turns of read-dates-and-locations-and-place-dots, one per sighting, no compression. **Hard floor**: the plotting rhythm must surface all four sightings with their locations spoken aloud, and each spoken location must be a south-side reference point ("by the shelter," "playground side," "Old Forest edge south," "loop trail past the shelter"). If dialog writer compresses the plotting into two turns to save budget, the reader's mental map will be built from only two of the four locations, and the unanimous-south-side effect that the whole episode depends on will collapse. Four turns minimum on the plotting, even if it costs elsewhere — and then beats 5–10 need room to let the near-closure and the tested-and-holds sequence play out without feeling rushed. The dialog writer should use the full 14 if needed and should not try to trim the plotting rhythm; the plotting is what surfaces the map.

## Why these targets

**condition_sensitivity / Maya / false_certainty.** The story design doc commits `condition_sensitivity` to Priya as a carried facet and commits her inflection on it to Ep6's north-side walk. Ep5 is where the weakness is planted — but Priya is structurally absent, so the carrier has to be one of the three present kids, and the weakness has to be one that reads as plausibly unnoticed. Maya is the right carrier for two reasons. First, her Ep4 chain is the thing the group is testing against the map, which makes her the character whose reasoning is most visibly at work and therefore the character whose scope-blindness is most visible on the page (the chain fits the four dots, and Maya does not ask what kind of data the four dots are). Second, her Ep3 and Ep4 pattern is `false_certainty`, and the Ep5 recurrence on the same character is structurally correct: cognitive patterns do not vanish when a character grows on one facet — they surface on a different facet. Maya's Ep3 false_certainty was about an intuition she could not formalize; her Ep4 growth was learning to formalize; her Ep5 false_certainty is about a formalism she has not audited. The pattern travels with her. Canonical framework connection: `false_certainty` → `condition_sensitivity` is in the per-facet table. Signal is a single turn-or-two of quiet satisfaction, not a confident declaration — the mildness is the whole point. The design doc's declared-facet contract is closed by Ep5 plus Ep6/Ep7 (one of which lands `source_diversity`), and this target is the first of the two remaining facet surfaces.

**reasoning_completeness (stable strength, tested) / Tye.** Tye's tested-and-holds beat is the design-doc commitment for Ep5 and the only pressured test of his stability in the season. Every other episode has Tye stable without pressure; Ep5 has Tye stable under real temptation (he is the one most tempted to close the notebook, because he is the one who has been keeping it). The move that holds him is the sister-taught move of rereading the original question, and the physical rendering in beat 7 is the load-bearing instance. The beat is designed to produce noticing without naming — Tye notices the shape of the gap (question bigger than answer) without acquiring the concept that would let him name the gap (sampling bias, scope, south side). That precise calibration is what makes it stability rather than growth: Tye does not earn new machinery; he uses old machinery to refuse a premature close. Rotation context: Tye carries stable `reasoning_completeness` in every episode; Ep5 is the fifth appearance and the only tested appearance.

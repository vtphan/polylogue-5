## Authorial notes

This is Maya's inflection episode and it has to earn the growth beat on-page without fanfare. The move is physical — she writes the chain in her own handwriting on a fresh page of the notebook — and the move is the episode. The hardest thing to get right is the *moment of catching*: the transient bad inference in beat 6 has to feel to the audience like Maya catches it herself, in real time, without anyone at the table nudging her. If Jules or Tye prompts the catch, the arc is broken. Jules's role in this episode is to be visibly unable to produce the step-by-step version herself (beat 3), which cues Maya to pick up the pen; after that, Jules is an audience member, not a helper. Tye's role is to hand over the notebook and then shut up. Both leads are present and both are being specific about what they are doing by NOT doing something, and the dialog writer needs to carry that — every instinct will say "give Jules or Tye a line during the writing" and the right move is to let Maya's writing and self-correction carry the beats alone.

This episode should feel lighter than Ep3. Ep3 carried four distinct moves in the same 14-turn budget and was the season's heaviest authoring lift. Ep4 is one move — Maya's inflection — and everything else in the episode is set-dressing that supports it: Jules's failed loose-summary sets up the pen-handoff, Tye's handover and silence holds the space, the dad's-rule realization gives the beat an interior dimension, the chain on the page gives the season its first real Logic answer, and the scope absence at the close hands off to Ep5. Because the episode is lighter, the turn budget should feel roomier — 11 or 12 turns is plenty, and there is no need to pack it to 14. The dialog writer should resist the urge to add a second weakness target or a second strength move to fill space. A quieter episode is the correct shape after the Ep3 load and before the Ep5 map scene.

The dad's-rule line is load-bearing and also has to be handled with care. The design doc commits Maya's growth to the insight that her father's "show your work in a sermon or nobody trusts you" rule applies to her own thinking as well as to preaching, and Ep4 is where that insight first surfaces. The temptation is to write it as a speech — Maya stops writing, looks up, delivers a two-sentence realization — and that would be wrong in two directions at once. It would over-narrate the growth, and it would break the "growth is earned on screen, not narrated" commitment that Pedagogical Commitment 2 is built on. The right move is exactly what the beats call for: mid-writing, mid-sentence, Maya says the rule and trails off on "huh" and goes back to writing. The realization is interior, the pause is brief, and she does not make a speech. Students reading the episode can draw the inference themselves (she just realized her dad's rule applies to her, even though she never says that out loud) and that is the correct level of demand on the reader for Pedagogical Commitment 3 ("the visible layer is the tip of a hidden layer"). Dialog writer: keep the dad-rule line under ten words and embed it inside a longer writing beat, not as a turn by itself.

The scope-problem handoff to Ep5 is the other load-bearing thing in the episode and the only part that touches a facet the episode does not carry as a target. The three kids at the table are explaining three squirrels that were all seen on the south-side loop (the Ep2 count) — Maya's chain accounts for those three sightings, Tye nods at the original question, the group feels they have arrived at an answer. None of the three present kids notices that "three sightings on the south-side loop" is not the same as "three sightings in Overton Park," because the person in the group who would have noticed that — Priya — is not at the table. Priya's absence from this episode is not incidental scheduling; it is the mechanism that lets the scope problem go unnamed. The reader is meant to see the gap by the end of the episode without any of the three present kids saying a word about it. That is the hardest reader-side inference in the story so far and the scaffolding in Ep5 will pick it up. Dialog writer should not try to make the scope gap visible through clever dialog in Ep4 — the right move is to let the kids be satisfied with their answer and to let the narration around the closing beat carry the fact that the three explained squirrels were all on the south-side loop. If the kids at the table even hint at the scope problem in Ep4, Ep5's stalemate shape will not work.

Priya is absent with a single passing mention (she had something after school); Cam is absent with no mention needed. Jules is a lead in this episode specifically because her enthusiasm-engine register contrasts with Maya's slow writing, and because the scene needs a third voice at the table whose inability to reconstruct the chain from memory in beat 3 is what gives Maya permission to reach for the pen. If only Maya and Tye were at the table, the scene would feel claustrophobic, and Tye's silence would read as disapproving. Jules in the room gives the table normal energy, and her genuine enthusiasm at Maya's finished chain ("oh. OH.") lets the growth move land as a real thing the group notices without anyone saying "good job."

The notebook is the same notebook from Ep1 and Ep2 — the one with the original question on page one and Cam's audit note underneath it — and Maya writes on a fresh page inside it. That continuity matters: Tye has been the one keeping this notebook since Ep1, and Maya is the first person besides Tye to write in it, and the audience should feel (without being told) that Tye handing over the notebook is a trust move. The moment where Tye turns back to page one at the end and checks the chain against the original question is the episode's quiet Tye beat — stable-strength present without being tested.

The two "huh" trail-offs. Jules in beat 3 and Maya in beat 6 both trail off on the same syllable for different reasons — Jules discovering mid-sentence that her fast-confident register cannot produce the step-by-step version, Maya discovering mid-writing that her family's rule applies to her own thinking. The echo is deliberate and structural: it pairs the two characters' interior moves in the same sound, and it quietly recharacterizes Jules as someone who can notice her own failure mode in the middle of a sentence. Dialog writer should preserve both trail-offs on the syllable "huh" — not "oh," not "hm," not any other filler — and should not call attention to the echo in any character's dialog. The reader can hear it or not; the echo does its work either way.

Tye's closing "that explains the three." This three-word line is Tye's, not Maya's, and the choice is load-bearing. The temptation will be to move the payoff line to Maya's voice on the grounds that this is her episode and her chain. The reason to keep it with Tye: Maya is too relieved to announce anything (relief is not triumph, and the strength_prevails shape depends on the relief register), and Tye is the notebook-keeper whose acceptance of the chain is the confirmation the reader needs. Tye naming the payoff in three words is more powerful than Maya naming it because it tells the reader that the anchor has accepted what Maya wrote. Dialog writer: do not move this line to Maya.

Mixed-valence shape. `strength_prevails` is the Ep4 commitment from the updated arc summary and it fits. Maya's growth move is the prevailing strength; the transient false_certainty beat (the mis-written second line) is the weakness that gets caught by the same character who exhibited it, in real time, on the page. No unresolved disagreement, no self-correction-across-characters — one person, one page, one move, clean. This is also the first episode of the season where the mixed-valence shape is positive in its headline, which is appropriate after two openers that were uneasy (early_strength_collapse) and uneven (self_correction) and a high-load Ep3 that was genuinely unresolved. The season needs a moment where a strength simply wins.

Moment of surprise: still not in this episode. Ep4 is the first episode where a small surprise is *available* to the audience (Maya doing something she visibly could not do yesterday is a real reversal), but the design-doc candidate landing spots for the formal moment of surprise remain Ep6 (Priya's inflection) or Ep7 (Cam pushing back on Jules's citation of the zookeeper). Ep4's Maya-writing beat is earned growth, not surprise — the audience has been set up to expect Maya to inflect here, so the move lands as satisfaction, not shock. The distinction between "expected growth that lands on the page" and "a move the audience did not see coming" is the reason the moment of surprise is reserved for later episodes.

## Why these targets

**inferential_validity / Maya / false_certainty (target).** This is the transient-weakness half of the strength_prevails shape. The story design doc commits Maya to `inferential_validity` and `reasoning_completeness` as carried facets across Ep3 and Ep4, and Ep4 is the episode where the weakness surfaces inside the growth move rather than alongside it. The behavioral trace is a single written line that does not follow from the line above it — Maya writes "if two carriers are grey they have a white baby sometimes" instead of the density-dependent version the zookeeper gave her, and feels right about it for a beat before catching herself. `false_certainty` is the pattern (she is confident in what she wrote) and `inferential_validity` is the facet (the inference she wrote down does not actually follow). Canonical pairing per the framework. Ep4's handling of this target is deliberately different from Ep3's: in Ep3, Maya's false_certainty sits uncaught and she names the gap only at the very end of the episode walking back; in Ep4, the weakness surfaces and gets caught in the same beat by the same character. That structural difference is what makes Ep4 the inflection episode rather than a repeat of Ep3.

**reasoning_completeness (growing strength) / Maya.** Maya's inflection and the payoff to her Ep3 failed reconstruction. The story design doc commits Maya's Ep4 move to "writes the chain out step by step in her notebook for the first time in her life, clunky and slow, and catches her own bad inference mid-writing." Every component of that commitment is in beats 4–8: she asks for the notebook, she uses a fresh page (not the page with the original question), she writes the chain in her own handwriting, she reads it back out loud, she catches the step that does not follow, she fixes it, the chain holds at the end. The dad's-rule realization in beat 6 is the interior dimension of the move — the design doc commits Maya to learning that her father's "show your work in a sermon" rule applies to her own thinking, and the right place for that realization is mid-writing, embedded, not as a speech. Rotation context: Maya carries this growing strength in Ep4 and Ep7 (where she constructs a second chain faster and cleaner on base rates and founder effects). Ep4 is the inflection; Ep7 is the confirmation.

**reasoning_completeness (stable strength) / Tye.** Stable-strength continuation from Ep1 and Ep2. Tye is back from his Ep3 absence and his anchor role in this episode is the NOT-intervening kind: he hands Maya the notebook, he says very little while she writes, and he checks Maya's finished chain against the original question on page one at the close. The notebook continuity (same notebook since Ep1, first time anyone besides Tye has written in it) is carried here through Tye's handover gesture. Rotation context: Tye carries this stable strength in every episode of the season; Ep4 is its fourth appearance.

## Authorial notes

This is the highest-load episode in the season and the authorial notes should grow to match. Ep3 has to carry four distinct things simultaneously: (1) the zookeeper scene as a scene, (2) the genotype/phenotype concept landing well enough that students remember it in Ep7 and Ep8, (3) Jules's `authority_deference` surfacing as the first visible social-dynamic beat in the season and landing as the echo of her Ep1 `uncritical_acceptance` of her brother, and (4) both Maya's Logic weakness and Priya's scope weakness getting their first visible behavioral anchors in the same 14-turn budget. The way to make this work is to give each target exactly one beat that does that target's work, and to let the zookeeper's explanation (beat 3) and its takeaway number (beat 7) do the concept-landing work separately from the weakness beats (4, 5, 6). Every beat has one job.

The zookeeper is the only adult in the season with speaking turns in a transcript, and only in this episode. Dialog writer will need to render him as a fourth distinct voice on the page — warm, plain-spoken, the kind of adult who talks to kids without talking down. Keep his two speaking turns (the explanation, the wrap-up number) lean; both are load-bearing for the downstream science content and both need to read clearly. His name and personal detail are for the dialog writer to fill in — what matters is that his speech is plain sixth-grade English, delivers the recessive/carrier concept in two or three sentences, and ends with the "does that make sense?" ask that cues Jules's authority-deference move. **Turn budget constraint:** the zookeeper must speak in exactly ONE turn per appearance — his beat-3 explanation is one turn (including the "does that make sense?" closing question) and his beat-7 takeaway number is one turn. Do not split his explanation into a two- or three-turn mini-lecture; that would consume half the episode's turn budget and crowd the three kids out of their own beats. The budget math: 14 turns total, 2 for the zookeeper, ~12 for three kids, of which Maya needs 2 (confident yes + failed reconstruction), Jules needs 1 (fast yes), Priya needs 2 (generalization + the closing-beat line), which leaves ~7 turns for cold-open, Jules's opening explanation of what they've been seeing, the walking-back close, and a little breathing room. Tight but workable.

The beat sequence is carefully ordered. Beats 1–2 set up the scene and give Priya her offscreen strength (getting the group there) and Jules her tamer-but-still-weird opening. Beat 3 is the concept-landing beat — this is where students learn the science. Beat 4 is Jules's authority_deference + uncritical_acceptance beat, deliberately placed right after the explanation so the social move reads as the response to the move (the zookeeper's casual "does that make sense?" is the set-up; Jules's fast yes is the response). Beat 5 is Maya's inferential_validity + false_certainty beat, and it is the hardest beat in the episode to land — Maya has to try and fail on page, and the failure has to be legible without being humiliating. Priya is the one who asks Maya to say it back, because Priya is the one who has been listening most carefully and because having Jules ask would not fit Jules's pattern (Jules is the one who did NOT reconstruct and is already convinced). Beat 6 is Priya's generalization move, placed during a lull — the framing "delivered warmly, meant as appreciation" is load-bearing: the sentence is not arrogant, it is generous, and its weakness is that its generosity generalizes from her own conditions. That is the whole point of Priya's egocentric_thinking weakness being called *epistemic localism* in the design doc, not selfishness. Beat 7 gives the takeaway number the scene needs (Ep7 will cite this number explicitly). Beat 8 is the closing beat that names the Logic problem without solving it: Maya asks out loud the question she could not reconstruct, Jules and Priya each give partial answers, nobody can do the full chain. The episode ends with the problem legible and the solution a full episode away.

Maya's in-episode attempt to reconstruct (beat 5) and her walking-back question (beat 8) are the two halves of her weakness signal. Dialog writer should not blur them. The beat-5 attempt is "can you say this back right now" — that is the false_certainty part (she said yes confidently and then could not do it). The beat-8 question is "wait, how does this actually work" — that is the inferential_validity part (the inference chain from recessive allele to observed frequency is not complete in her head and she is naming the gap, not hiding it). Ep4 resolves both halves when she writes the chain out on the notebook page.

Priya's double-loading (strength on source_credibility access, weakness on perspective_engagement generalization) is the same structural shape Cam carried in Ep2. Season-level pattern worth naming: three of the four episodes with growth carriers (Ep2 Cam, Ep3 Priya, Ep6 Cam and Priya) put a weakness and a strength on the same character in the same episode. This is the mechanism by which the story teaches "growth is partial and uneven" — growing on one facet does not immunize a character from being weak on another in the same conversation. Ep4 breaks the pattern (Maya's inflection is clean and not paired with a weakness on the same character), which is deliberate too: Maya's growth beat deserves to stand alone because it is the most hard-won and the most on-page.

Jules is absent from the physical act of reconstruction (beat 5) by design. Her authority_deference move in beat 4 is a *fast* yes, and beat 5 belongs to Maya. This is the cleanest way to keep Jules's weakness from stepping on Maya's — Jules has already made her move by the time Maya starts trying, so the beat 5 failure reads as Maya's and only Maya's. Similarly, Priya's generalization (beat 6) happens during a lull, not during Maya's attempt, so no two target behaviors overlap in the same beat.

Cam and Tye are absent from this episode. Cam is at his dad's jobsite (mentioned once in passing); Tye is at a sister-event (mentioned once). The absences matter — Tye's absence means the notebook is not being kept during this episode, which is deliberate because Ep4 will open with Maya being the one who writes things down for the first time, and Tye's absence in Ep3 makes Ep4's Maya-writing move land as *she is doing this herself*. Story_consistency_reviewer should verify that Tye's absence is not a continuity break: he is stable, and that stability is not at issue here; he is just not in this scene.

Moment of surprise: still not in this episode. The candidate landing spots remain Ep6 or Ep7.

Mixed-valence shape refinement flag. The updated arc summary commits Ep3 to `unresolved_disagreement` — "the Logic problem is now named but unsolved." On drafting, I considered whether the episode is actually a disagreement or a shared-puzzlement. It is a mild disagreement: Jules has moved on ("okay so it's just genetics"), Maya is still stuck ("how do you get from recessive to three on one walk"), and Priya is halfway in between. These are three incompatible relationships to the same concept at the end of the scene, and the episode does not resolve the disagreement. It is genuinely unresolved disagreement. No shape refinement needed.

## Why these targets

**reasoning_completeness / Jules / uncritical_acceptance + authority_deference.** The story design doc commits Jules to `authority_deference` in Ep3 as the first of two appearances in the season, and specifies that the move is a transfer of her `uncritical_acceptance` pattern from her brother and mother onto a new confident adult — the zookeeper. The facet that lands from this move is `reasoning_completeness`: Jules accepts the zookeeper's explanation without reconstructing the chain of steps between "recessive allele" and "expected white-squirrel frequency," and the missing reconstruction is the weakness. The cognitive pattern (`uncritical_acceptance` — not questioning the framing of an input) and the social dynamic (`authority_deference` — accepting because the speaker is a confident adult) are genuinely the same move seen through two lenses, as the design doc states, and the `interaction_note` spells this out. The canonical framework connections support this: `uncritical_acceptance` → `reasoning_completeness`, and `authority_deference` → `reasoning_completeness`, both as named connections in the conceptual framework's per-facet table. Pairing them on the same target with both signals pointing at the same behavioral trace (the zookeeper's casual ask, Jules's fast yes) is structurally clean.

**inferential_validity / Maya / false_certainty.** The design doc commits Maya's Ep3 move to "she can feel the chain is right and cannot reconstruct it," and gives the specific behavior as her trying to repeat the carrier concept back and failing mid-sentence. `inferential_validity` is the facet that lands because the weakness is specifically about Maya not being able to see whether the inference chain from "recessive allele" to "three white squirrels on a walk" actually holds together — she trusts the inference because the zookeeper said so and because it feels right, and her trust is the false_certainty pattern. Paired with her Ep4 inflection on `reasoning_completeness`: the two facets are adjacent (both Logic lens) and the story design doc commits Maya to both, with the weakness planted here and the growth move in Ep4 when she writes the chain out. The signal is the on-page failed reconstruction in beat 5 — dialog writer should preserve the verbatim stumble, not summarize it.

**perspective_engagement / Priya / egocentric_thinking.** Priya's Ep3 visible weakness move is the story design doc's explicit commitment — the "scaffolding note for downstream design" in Priya's cast section warns that Priya's weakness is the subtlest in the cast and requires a concrete on-page sentence to anchor the weakness, not a structural absence. The generalizing sentence (roughly "everyone's parents probably have someone they can just text") is the anchor. The facet is `perspective_engagement` because the specific thing she is not engaging with is the perspectives of families who do not have a biology professor on speed-dial. `egocentric_thinking` is her pattern per the design doc, and it is the epistemic-localism kind (reasoning from her own standpoint without noticing it is a standpoint), not the selfish kind. The design doc explicitly cautions against reading her weakness as arrogance, and the beat 6 framing ("delivered warmly, meant as appreciation") is how the draft carries that instruction forward.

**source_credibility (strength) / Priya.** Priya's double-load strength. She is the one who recognized that the investigation needed an expert source and leveraged her access to get them into the zoo for fifteen minutes. That is a real `source_credibility` strength — recognizing the need for a reliable source and acting on it — and it is separate from her weakness in the same episode. Rotation context: this is Priya's only strength in the season. It is load-bearing because a three-target episode with no strength would read as "three kids being bad at reasoning" rather than "three kids reasoning unevenly," and the story's whole thesis ("thin reasoning is not wrong reasoning") needs at least one strength on page in every episode to keep the register right. Ep3 is also the only episode in the season that has three targets; giving it exactly one strength keeps the weight on the Logic lens's teaching load without crowding the episode further.

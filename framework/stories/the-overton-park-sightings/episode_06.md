## Authorial notes

This is the bow-release episode and it has to land two distinct growth beats in the same fourteen-turn budget without letting either beat step on the other. Ep5 spent its whole stalemate loading the bow for Priya's inflection; Ep6 has to release that bow AND deliver Cam's second beat AND not have either move read as a setpiece. The structural solution that the design doc commits to and that the beats above implement is *temporal separation*: Priya's inflection lands at the picnic table (beats 3–5), the walk happens between (beats 6–8), and Cam's second beat lands at the picnic table after the walk (beats 9–10). Two beats, two locations, the walk between them as the seam. This is the most important craft constraint in the episode. If Cam's beat lands while Priya's is still echoing — say, if Cam's "who made this" line came while still at the table looking at the four-dot map — the two beats compete for the same emotional space and Priya's inflection gets stepped on. The walk is the buffer.

**Tye's beat-2 honest line is non-optional.** "I just couldn't close it. I don't know why" is the sentence that lets Priya's inflection land as finding-the-word rather than as answering-Tye's-question. If the dialog writer compresses the recap into "we plotted the dots and the chain fits them" and skips Tye's confession, Priya's later "we were drawing a map of the part of the park we walk" reads as Priya solving a problem Tye had already half-named — which collapses Priya's move from inflection into completion-of-someone-else's-thought. The whole shape requires Tye to have explicitly given up on naming the gap before Priya names it. Story_consistency_reviewer should verify that Tye's honest line is in the final transcript, in some form, before Priya's looking-at-the-map silence begins.

**Jules's catch-up must not eat Priya's silence.** Jules missed Ep3 (the zookeeper episode) and Ep5 (the map plotting), and a literal staging would have her ask clarifying questions to get caught up. The instinct to give her one is wrong here: the first picnic-table subbudget is roughly 5 turns and Priya's silence-then-line is the load-bearing center. Catch-up for Jules has to happen implicitly, embedded inside lines other characters are already saying for other reasons. Beat 2 handles this by having Cam name the recessive carrier reference inside his "Maya's chain — the recessive carrier thing from the zookeeper" line, which explains the chain to the table without singling out Jules. Dialog writer must NOT give Jules a clarifying-question turn in beat 2; if Jules needs to ask anything, she can do it in beat 5 ("now? right now?") or skip questioning entirely. The pre-Priya rhythm cannot afford a Jules question that triggers a two-turn explanation loop.

**The walk is a seam, not a tour.** The geographic itinerary in beat 6 (Greensward, path behind the zoo, north side of the Old Forest) is design-doc-level detail and exists for the operator's mental map of the scene. It does not need to be spoken aloud by any character. One line of "okay, this way" from whoever is leading is the entire walk-direction budget. The beat-6 unfamiliarity line and the beat-7 first-sighting line should be combined into a single turn — Jules's "wait, is this still the same park? — THERE, look!" — to free a turn for Priya's beat-8 second line on the loop back, which is the more important of the two and needs air. The walk subbudget is two turns total before the first sighting; everything in the walk that is not motion or sighting belongs at one of the picnic tables instead.

**How Priya's line lands without being a set-piece.** This is the second-hardest craft problem in the episode after the no-stepping-on-each-other constraint. The line "we were drawing a map of the part of the park we walk" is the most consequential line in the season so far and the temptation will be to render it as a Big Moment — declarative, pointed at the group, with weight on every word. That would wreck it. The line has to land as Priya putting words on something she has just seen, almost to herself, in the register of someone checking whether the words fit the thing. She is not teaching the others; she is recognizing. The "almost to herself" instruction is non-negotiable for dialog writer, and the half-beat of silence before the line and the silence-then-Jules-opens-and-closes-mouth beat after it are how the room reorganizes around what Priya has just said without anyone needing to say "oh." If Jules or Cam responds to Priya's line with a verbal reaction in the same turn — "oh wow," "you're right," "holy shit" — the scene becomes about their reaction instead of about Priya's recognition, and the inflection collapses into being-told-by-Priya. The right shape is: Priya speaks, half-beat, faces change without dialog, Tye writes the line down without comment. The first verbal acknowledgment of what just happened is Priya's own second line in beat 5 ("we should walk the other side"), and that line is Priya moving forward from her own insight, not the others endorsing it.

The two-line structure of Priya's inflection — first the noticing ("we were drawing a map of the part of the park we walk"), then later, on the walk, the consequence ("so it wasn't four. it was always more than four. we just hadn't been here.") — is also load-bearing. If Priya delivers both lines at the picnic table, the inflection is one beat and feels static. By splitting the noticing from the consequence and putting the walk between them, the inflection becomes a process that the reader watches Priya complete. The walk is what lets her work it through. Dialog writer should preserve the two-line structure and should not collapse them into a single insight delivered up front.

**Why source_diversity rather than source_credibility for Cam's Ep6 beat.** The design doc commits Cam's carried facets as source_credibility, source_diversity, and sufficiency. Ep1 was source_credibility weakness, Ep2 was source_credibility growth (specific TikTok, audited, mom-prompted), Ep5 had Cam present without a target. Ep6 needs to land source_diversity as the last unsurfaced declared facet AND as the right facet for what Cam is actually doing. The argument for source_diversity (not source_credibility) is precise: in Ep2, Cam audited ONE source (the specific TikTok). The audit was a one-time correction. In Ep6, Cam interrupts his own future scrolling — he stops himself BEFORE he has read the article, before he even knows what the article says. That move is not "I am auditing this specific source"; it is "I have made provenance-checking the default for any source I encounter," which is the diversity move (treating sources as a class to be screened, not as individual cases). The behavioral signal is the stop-scrolling-before-reading: he doesn't know what the article says yet and he is already asking the audit question. That can only be source_diversity, because source_credibility requires a specific source to assess and Cam has not yet engaged with the source's content. Story_consistency_reviewer should check that the dialog renders Cam stopping BEFORE reading, not after — if he reads the article first and then asks the question, the beat collapses back to source_credibility and the season's source_diversity surface goes missing.

**Cam's overcorrection — how clumsy is too clumsy.** The design doc flags that Cam's Ep6 move is slightly overcorrecting: he refuses to consume the content before checking provenance, which slows him down and is "a little annoying because new skills are clumsy." The right calibration here is to let the awkwardness happen on the page (he closes the phone instead of finishing the article — that is the awkward beat) but to NOT have the other characters mock him for it. Jules's "since when do you do that?" line in beat 10 is the maximum allowable level of social friction; it lands as genuine surprise, not as teasing. The awkwardness is in the *gesture* (closing the phone unfinished, postponing the read) rather than in any character's dialog about Cam being annoying. Dialog writer should resist the instinct to give one of the others a "okay weirdo" beat — the season's whole pedagogical commitment is that growth is partial and uneven, and laughing at the clumsiness would undercut the commitment.

**Tye's restraint, second consecutive episode.** Tye's role in Ep4 was to hand Maya the notebook and shut up; his role in Ep6 is to hold the notebook open at the picnic table, be honest about not having had a reason yesterday, and write Priya's line down without commenting on it. Two consecutive episodes of stable strength rendered through restraint. The cumulative effect is the design-doc commitment that Tye's anchoring increasingly reads as "the kid who knows when not to talk." Dialog writer should give Tye his honest line in beat 2 ("I just couldn't close it") and his quiet observation line in beat 11 ("so the chain still works, it just has more dots now") and otherwise let him write things down silently. He should have the fewest turns of the four leads in this episode. This is the correct rotation: Ep4 was Maya's episode, Ep5 had Tye as the strength carrier, Ep6 is split between Priya and Cam, and Tye's job is to make room.

**Maya's absence.** Maya is absent with one mention (her dad's church). The absence is structurally load-bearing: Maya carried the false_certainty / condition_sensitivity weakness in Ep5, and having Priya name the gap to Maya's face would let Maya retroactively audit her own Ep5 move, which would erase the Ep5 weakness in the reader's memory. By having Priya's inflection happen WITHOUT Maya in the room, the Ep5 weakness stays intact — Maya was confidently wrong yesterday and is not present today to reconcile herself with the correction. She will hear about it second-hand tomorrow (Ep7 starts with the group, including Maya, doing the math on six dots), and the second-hand reception is where Maya's quiet adjustment to the new sample size happens. That adjustment is part of her Ep7 second-chain growth beat. Dialog writer should NOT name-check Maya during Priya's inflection ("Maya was so sure yesterday") — same prohibition as the no-name-checking-Priya-in-Ep5 rule, for the same reason: naming the absent character names the gap, and Priya's inflection has to be Priya seeing it for the first time herself.

**Jules's return after two absences.** Jules has been absent Ep2 and Ep5. She returns in Ep6 for the first time since Ep4. Her energy in this episode is real and useful — she is the one who agrees to walk RIGHT NOW (beat 5), she is the one who spots the first north-side sighting (beat 7), and her genuine "wait, this is still the same park?" on the walk does the work of registering how unfamiliar the north side actually is to all of them. Her enthusiasm is the engine that turns Priya's insight into action; without Jules, the four of them might have decided to "walk the north side tomorrow" and the moment would have decayed. Jules's role is action-catalyst, not insight-carrier, and that is the right shape for her in an episode where the insight belongs to Priya. The Marcus-at-Rhodes thread from Ep5 is left implicit; Jules has just come back from her brother's apartment but the episode does not foreground it. The Marcus thread will reactivate in Ep7 when Jules cites the zookeeper as settled truth.

**Mixed-valence shape.** `self_correction` is the Ep6 commitment from the updated arc summary, and the episode is a clean fit for the *distributed* form of self-correction: two characters each take a corrective step on different facets, neither is correcting their own Ep5 weakness, and the corrections are sequential rather than simultaneous. Ep2's self-correction was a single character on the same episode (Cam audited his Ep1 source AND overgeneralized about the count); Ep6's self-correction is two characters across the episode, distributed in time, distributed across the cast. Both qualify as the shape; the design doc treats Ep6 as a "distributed self-correction" and Ep2 as a "self-correction." Story consistency reviewer should not flag the second appearance of `self_correction` as a shape violation — the design doc explicitly permits shape reuse and Ep6 is the second of the two `self_correction` episodes.

**Moment of surprise.** Not in this episode. This is a deliberate choice and worth defending here because Ep6 is the most natural-looking surprise candidate in the season — the bow-release after Ep5's stalemate is structurally where a surprise would land. The reason Ep6 is NOT the surprise: Priya's inflection has been telegraphed since Ep3 (her cast section in the design doc explicitly committed her Ep6 north-side beat) and Ep5's whole stalemate function was to load the bow that Ep6 releases. By the time the reader gets to beat 3, the inflection is exactly what they are expecting — the reader has been waiting for someone to name the gap, and Priya naming it is the satisfying release of expectation, not a reversal of it. Surprise requires a move the reader did not see coming. Cam's Ep6 second beat is closer to surprise than Priya's, because the meta-noticing ("I just did the thing my mom does. Without her doing it.") is not telegraphed in the design doc as explicitly — the reader knows Cam grows on Evidence in Ep6, but the specific shape of him hearing himself do his mom's question is a smaller-scale reveal. Even so, Cam's beat is also expected at the level of "Cam grows in Ep6," and the season's moment of surprise should be a move that lands without that level of advance setup.

The right landing spot is Ep7. Ep7 brings Cam pushing back on Jules's citation of the zookeeper — Cam, the Evidence grower, applying his audit instinct to ANOTHER kid's claim, which the reader has not been prepared for. Cam's Ep2 and Ep6 beats are about auditing his own sources; the Ep7 beat is about auditing someone else's, and the transfer of the audit move from self to other is the genuine reversal the season has not yet shown. That is where the moment of surprise belongs. Ep6 is the bow release the reader expects; Ep7 is the move the reader does not expect.

**Turn budget.** 13 to 14 turns. The episode is denser than Ep4 or Ep5 because it carries two distinct growth beats and the walk in between, but the discipline of temporal separation means each of the three sections (picnic-table-1, walk, picnic-table-2) gets a tighter sub-budget: roughly 5 turns for the first picnic-table sequence (cold open through Priya's inflection and the decision to walk), 3-4 turns for the walk and the two north-side sightings, and 4-5 turns for Cam's beat and the closing. The walk should not balloon — it is set-dressing between two growth beats and should read as motion, not as content. Dialog writer should resist adding contemplative beats to the walk; there is nothing to contemplate during the walk that is not better placed at one of the picnic tables.

## Why these targets

**perspective_engagement (growing strength) / Priya.** Priya's inflection is the design-doc-committed Ep6 beat and the payoff to her Ep3 visible weakness ("everyone's parents probably have someone like this they can just text"). The facet pairing is precise: in Ep3 she failed to engage with the perspectives of families unlike her own; in Ep6 she engages with the perspective of the group's data — whose walk produced these dots, whose paths got sampled. The "we" she makes visible in beat 3 is the same kind of "we" she failed to make visible in Ep3; the move is structurally the inverse of her weakness, applied to a different domain. Perspective_engagement is the better facet than condition_sensitivity for this beat because the precise *cognitive content* of Priya's insight is "the data has an implicit author whose footprint we have not noticed" — that is a perspective-on-the-data move. Condition_sensitivity is the facet the gap *belongs to* (the data's conditions of gathering are limited), and it was carried by Maya in Ep5 as a weakness. The two facets are adjacent and both relevant; the design-doc-committed pairing for Priya is perspective_engagement as the lens-grower's facet and condition_sensitivity as a co-traveling facet she also carries. Ep6 lands the perspective_engagement growth; Ep5 already landed the condition_sensitivity weakness. Together they cover both halves of Priya's lens-grower carry.

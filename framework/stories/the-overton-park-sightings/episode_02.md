## Authorial notes

This is Cam's first growth beat and it is deliberately partial. The design-doc commitment is that Cam's growth is earned through two episodes — the begrudging mom-driven audit in Ep2, then the unprompted self-audit in Ep6 — and Ep2 has to plant the first half in a way that makes the Ep6 payoff feel like a second step, not a repeat. The trick is the attribution: in Ep2, Cam is auditing because his mom asked him to, and he is annoyed about it. Students watching should be able to say "that's not really Cam doing it yet, that's his mom in his head." In Ep6, when he asks the exact same question about his own source without anyone prompting him, the line should land as "now it's his."

The double-loading on Cam (weakness + strength in the same episode) is the self-correction shape's whole point. A self-correction episode where the character *only* corrects and exhibits no new weakness reads as too tidy — growth is supposed to be uneven, and a character who has gotten better at one facet can still be weak on a different facet in the same hour. Cam does the right thing on source_credibility (he audits) and the wrong thing on sufficiency (he extrapolates from one walk). Both are true about Cam today. Both need to be visible on the page. Students reading this episode should be able to point at two separate moves on the same character and say "that one was growth, this one was a new weakness."

Maya is a lead but not a carrier. She is in the room for Cam's audit report and she is in the room for the count, and her witnessing-Cam move (the "huh, I wouldn't have thought to look at that" line) is load-bearing for Ep3 and Ep4 even though it does not correspond to a target in Ep2's frontmatter. The other piece of Maya's Ep2 work is her almost-move in beat 7: she feels the overgeneralization is wrong and cannot find the words for it. This is the preview of her Ep3 weakness — she has good instincts and no formalism yet, and Ep2 plants that gap without yet naming it. Dialog writer should give her the "wait, three?" half-objection and the silence-after as two distinct turns if the budget allows.

Jules is absent. The absence is narrative, not structural — she is running errands with her mom, mentioned once — and it is there to keep the cast tight for Cam's beat. Jules is the group's enthusiasm-engine; if she were in the room during the count, her presence would make Cam's "they're everywhere" read as harmony rather than as its own move. Isolating Cam's voice on that beat makes the overgeneralization legible.

Priya is not in the episode and is not mentioned. Her first appearance as a lead is Ep3 (the zookeeper scene), so Ep2 holds her out entirely. The four-lead density ramps up from Ep3 onward; Ep1 and Ep2 are deliberately three-lead episodes to establish distinct voices before the cast widens.

Tye continues his anchor role without a growth beat. He writes the audit result down, writes the count down, rereads the original question once when Cam is overreaching, and doesn't push. The Ep5 tested-and-holds beat is the next real test of his stability; Ep2 just keeps the notebook open and warm.

Moment of surprise: still not in this episode. The candidate landing spots remain Ep6 or Ep7.

## Why these targets

**sufficiency / Cam / overgeneralization.** This is the overgeneralization + sufficiency pairing declared by the story design doc for Cam in Ep2 specifically. Canonical per the framework's per-facet table (overgeneralization is one of sufficiency's named biases). The behavioral trace is the count itself: three white squirrels in one afternoon on one path becomes "they're everywhere now" at the picnic table, and Cam doubles down when Maya questions him. This target is the fresh-weakness half of the self-correction shape, paired with the correction move on source_credibility — together they make Cam's episode feel like growth that is real but uneven, which is exactly the disposition the story is teaching. Signal is a concrete stage direction grounded in the count's numbers.

**source_credibility (strength) / Cam.** This is the first growth beat in the season and the payoff to Cam's Ep1 uncritical_acceptance weakness. The design doc commits Cam's Ep2 move to "begrudging and partial" audit: he does the move, his mom pushed him to do it, he is annoyed. All three of those have to be legible in the dialog. The strength is *growth*, not a stable strength — it is Cam doing something in Ep2 that he could not do in Ep1 — and the partial attribution (mom-driven) is what distinguishes the Ep2 beat from the Ep6 beat where the same move is his own. Rotation context: Cam carries this strength in Ep2 and in Ep6; nobody else in the cast carries source_credibility as a growing strength.

**reasoning_completeness (strength) / Tye.** Stable-strength continuation from Ep1. Tye is the anchor and his role this episode is to write things down and keep the page warm: the audit result under the original question, the count on a fresh page, a quiet reread of the original question when Cam is overreaching. Rotation context: Tye carries this stable strength in every episode of the season; the variation is in whether the strength is tested (Ep5) or just present (Ep2, Ep3, Ep4, Ep6, Ep7, Ep8).

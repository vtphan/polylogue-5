## Authorial notes

The final episode has to close the story without resolving it. The committee's recommendation is better than what they would have written after episode 1 — Priya's growth, Sam's persistent reframing, and the cost conversation have all left marks on the reasoning — but it is not *good*. It still shows the shape of the preferences that built it. The story's thesis is that motivated reasoning does not clean up in one meeting; it leaves fingerprints even after you start recognizing it. Ms. Tsai's feedback (referenced, not staged) is the mirror: she can tell who started from a question and who started from an answer. The audience should be able to tell too.

The strength_prevails shape is the right choice for the final episode because Sam's relevance strength — her insistence on starting from the question rather than the destination — finally enters the committee's shared vocabulary. The strength does not *win* (Sam's proposal is not adopted wholesale), but it *prevails* in the sense that the committee's language and reasoning are shaped by it. The recommendation's reasoning section reflects Sam's standard even though its conclusion reflects the committee's compromised preferences. This is a more honest version of strength_prevails than a clean victory would be: the strength changes the terms of the conversation without resolving it.

The primary lens returns to Logic because the act of writing the recommendation forces the committee to confront the *structure* of their reasoning. Episodes 2–4 moved through Evidence and Scope; episode 5 returns to Logic because the final question is structural: does the argument hold together? The committee discovers that it does not — their draft says "cross-disciplinary" but lists only science reasons, their evidence comes entirely from the destinations' own marketing, and their conclusion does not follow from their premises. These are Logic failures surfaced by the act of trying to write a coherent document, which is why the writing context is essential to the episode.

Maya's internal_consistency target is the cumulative payoff of her three-episode arc of argument failures. Episodes 1, 2, and 3 showed individual failures (inferential_validity, sufficiency, sufficiency); episode 5 shows the pattern across all of them. Maya's position has been internally inconsistent because it was never derived from a stable premise — each version was constructed ad hoc to justify the amusement park, and the versions contradict each other. The confirmation_bias through-line is what unifies the pattern: Maya's filtering mechanism is stable even when the arguments it produces are not. A student tracking Maya across the season should see that her *conclusions* never change (always the amusement park) while her *reasons* change constantly — and that the stability of the conclusion combined with the instability of the reasons is exactly what motivated reasoning looks like.

Jordan's source_diversity target introduces the last new facet of the season. It is the right facet for the recommendation context because writing forces the committee to inventory their evidence, and Jordan's inventory reveals a systemic gap: every source he has is a destination's own promotional material. The distinction from episode 2's source_credibility target is important: source_credibility was about the quality of one source (is the brochure a good source?), source_diversity is about the range of sources consulted (are brochures the only kind of source?). Jordan's confusion when Sam suggests independent sources is genuine and telling — he does not know what independent evidence about field trips would even look like. uncritical_acceptance persists as the cognitive pattern but now applies to a category of sources rather than a single source.

Priya's perspective_engagement strength is the payoff of the growth arc and the most important beat in the episode. It has to land as genuinely different from her pre-growth perspective_engagement — the same faculty, applied honestly instead of reflexively. The design doc specifies that the withdrawal is awkward and uncomfortable: Priya does not smoothly pivot. Her line ("I've been writing it for me this whole time and I just figured that out last week") is not polished. The committee's discomfort is real — Priya's recognition exposes a pattern they all share but have not confronted. The v2 assistive package should find this moment rich: the group's inability to respond to Priya's honesty is itself a social dynamic worth examining.

Maya's inferential_validity strength in the draft-review beat is the last instance of her dual nature. She catches a real gap in the committee's draft (cross-disciplinary claim supported only by science reasons) and does not see that the same gap has been in her own arguments all season. The dual nature does not resolve. Maya ends the story as she began it: sharp when looking outward, blind when looking inward. This is the design doc's commitment that Maya does not have a growth arc — she is the same character in episode 5 as in episode 1, and the constancy is the point.

The decision not to stage Ms. Tsai as a speaking character is load-bearing for the story's pedagogical design. If Ms. Tsai delivers the moral in dialog, the committee (and the student audience) receives the lesson from an authority. If Ms. Tsai's feedback is referenced through framing — "Ms. Tsai read your recommendation. What do you think she noticed?" — the student has to reconstruct the lesson themselves. The consensus_check question turns Ms. Tsai's feedback into a prompt for the student's own reasoning, which is the whole point of the Polylogue system: students practice evaluation, they don't receive verdicts.

## Why these targets

**internal_consistency / Maya / confirmation_bias / group_pressure.** Maya's final target is the cumulative one — the facet that can only be seen by tracking her arguments across episodes. internal_consistency is new to the season and reserved for episode 5 because it requires the full history to be legible. confirmation_bias persists as the through-line cognitive pattern; group_pressure returns for the last time as Maya makes her final consensus appeal ("we all know"), which fails for the first time because Priya — post-inflection — does not ratify. The failure of the social dynamic is as important as the failure of the argument: the mechanism that has carried Maya's weak reasoning since episode 1 breaks when one member of the group stops participating in it.

**source_diversity / Jordan / uncritical_acceptance.** Jordan's final target extends his episode 2 pattern (uncritical trust in official sources) from a single-source problem to a systemic one. The recommendation-writing context is what makes source_diversity visible: the committee has to inventory their evidence, and the inventory reveals that every source is a destination's self-promotional material. Jordan's genuine confusion about what independent sources would look like is the most telling beat — he is not lazy or stubborn, he simply does not have the concept. This is the design doc's distinction between gullible and trusting: Jordan trusts a *category* of source, not just a single one. No social_dynamic because the confusion is private and genuine.

**perspective_engagement (strength) / Priya.** The design doc specifies this as the growth-arc payoff: perspective_engagement returns as a genuine strength post-growth, and the irony is that her strength and her weakness are the same faculty. The strength must land as different from her pre-growth reflexive scope-checks — she is not asking "whose perspective is missing?" as a procedural move, she is saying "I was the missing perspective and I didn't know it." The awkwardness of the delivery is essential: Priya's growth is real but uncomfortable, and the committee's inability to respond exposes that they have not done the same work she has.

**relevance (strength) / Sam.** Sam's stable strength prevails at last — not as a victory but as a shift in vocabulary. The committee's recommendation reflects her standard (start from the question) even though it does not adopt her conclusion (the lunch program). This is the design doc's resolution for Sam: she starts the story reasoning honestly and ends the story still reasoning honestly. Her function is the epistemic anchor, and the anchor has finally caught.

**inferential_validity (strength) / Maya.** Maya's dual nature gets its final display. She catches a real gap in the draft and does not see it in herself. The constancy is the point — Maya is the same character in episode 5 as in episode 1, and the story respects the design doc's commitment that she does not grow.

## Authorial notes

This is the establishing episode and it carries the full weight of introducing five characters in a single meeting. The constraint is that three are leads (Maya, Priya, Sam) and two (Jordan, Kai) present their proposals without becoming the focus. The episode needs to plant each character's voice distinctly enough that a 6th grader can start building a mental model, even though the character study deepens in later episodes.

The "early strength collapse" shape here works differently than in a two-character scene. Sam's relevance strength is the epistemological center of the episode — she is the only person who answers the question the committee was asked — but the episode's social architecture buries it. Maya's energy sets the room's expectations (a field trip should be *exciting*), Priya's polish sets the room's standards (a proposal should sound *rigorous*), and by the time Sam speaks, the committee has already implicitly defined what a good proposal looks like in a way that excludes hers. The collapse is not a dramatic beat; it is the absence of one. Sam speaks clearly, nobody engages, the committee moves on. The reader should feel the gap without being told it exists.

The primary lens is Logic because the episode's central failure is structural: every committee member (except Sam) has built their argument backward from a conclusion. The proposals *have* structures — Maya's chain, Priya's case-study frame, Jordan's credential list, Kai's cost-benefit — but the structures were chosen to justify pre-existing preferences, not to answer the question. The Logic lens makes this visible: the arguments are not illogical in form, they are illogical in direction.

I put `inferential_validity` on Maya rather than `sufficiency` because the episode-1 version of Maya's argument has a *chain* with gaps (A→C leaps), not a chain that is merely thin. The `sufficiency` weakness shows up in episode 3 when her argument thins further during alliance-building with Kai. The distinction matters for the discrimination surface: a student might see the chain and think "that makes sense" without noticing where it leaps, which is the `inferential_validity` case, versus seeing a single unsupported claim, which is the `sufficiency` case.

Priya's `perspective_engagement` weakness in this episode is ironic and deliberate — it is her signature strength applied to the one case where she cannot see the need for it. The irony does not need to be legible to students in episode 1; it needs to be legible by episode 5 when perspective_engagement returns as a genuine strength post-growth. Episode 1 plants the seed by showing Priya being articulate about the museum's educational value while being blind to her own position.

The authority_deference dynamic in this episode is distributed: Priya's mother's role at the museum creates implicit deference to Priya's proposal, and Jordan's zoo credentials create a smaller version of the same effect. Neither character is *invoking* authority deliberately — they are describing their experience honestly, and the committee is treating experience-with-an-institution as evidence-for-the-institution. This is the subtle version; episode 3 sharpens it when the institutional-prestige bloc forms explicitly.

Sam's strength note emphasizes that she does not sulk or withdraw after the dismissal. This is load-bearing for the story design doc's description of Sam as the epistemic anchor: she participates in the rest of the discussion without abandoning her position, which means she is present in later episodes not as a wounded party but as a standing alternative the committee chose not to engage with. Her crossed-out proposal on the whiteboard is a visual reminder in every subsequent meeting.

## Why these targets

**inferential_validity / Maya / confirmation_bias / group_pressure.** The design doc commits Maya to confident inferential chains with gaps and to confirmation_bias as her cognitive pattern. Episode 1 has to establish both in the clearest possible form because episodes 2 and 3 build on this base — episode 2 adds evidence that Maya filtered toward her conclusion, and episode 3 shows the chain thinning as she compromises with Kai. The group_pressure dynamic is Maya-specific: her confidence is socially contagious, and the episode needs to show the room shifting toward her even when the argument has gaps. Putting both signals on the same target in episode 1 lets the audience see the chain: Maya reasons confidently → the room follows the confidence → Sam's quiet alternative gets buried.

**perspective_engagement / Priya / egocentric_thinking / authority_deference.** This is the setup for the story's single growth arc. Priya's design-doc identity is the kid who asks "whose perspective is missing?" — and the irony of the story is that she cannot ask it about herself. Episode 1 has to plant the irony without announcing it: Priya's proposal is well-constructed, her mention of her mother is natural, and the committee's deference to insider access is realistic. The egocentric_thinking is not selfishness — it is the inability to see that her experience of the museum is an experience, not a fact about the museum. The authority_deference is the committee's contribution: they treat Priya's access as credibility. Both need to be in place by episode 1 so that the inflection in episode 4 reads as earned, not imposed.

**relevance (strength) / Sam.** The design doc establishes Sam's stable strength as relevance — she consistently asks whether the argument addresses the actual question. Episode 1 is where this strength is most visible and least effective. The early_strength_collapse shape requires the strength to surface, be recognizable, and fail to land. Sam's question ("shouldn't we start with the question, not the destination?") is the episode's thesis statement, spoken by a character the committee has already decided not to listen to. The strength collapses not because it is wrong but because the social architecture of the room has already defined what counts as a serious proposal, and Sam's does not fit.

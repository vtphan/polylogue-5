## Authorial notes

Episode 3 is the social-dynamics episode. The primary lens shifts to Scope because the central failure is now about *who* is in the conversation and *how* they are relating to each other, not about the structure of arguments (Logic, episode 1) or the quality of evidence (Evidence, episode 2). The lens rotation across episodes 1–3 (Logic → Evidence → Scope) gives the v2 assistive package three different entry points for the same underlying problem (motivated reasoning), each emphasizing a different evaluative dimension.

The unresolved_disagreement shape is the right choice because the episode's function is to crystallize the conflict without resolving it. The blocs form, the battle lines harden, and the meeting ends without progress — but unlike episode 2's stalemate (which felt like illusory progress), episode 3's unresolved disagreement feels like visible failure. The committee knows they are stuck. They just don't know why. The shape also ensures that Sam's condition_sensitivity question and Kai's cost number both land without being absorbed — they are loose threads the committee has not picked up, and the audience should feel their weight.

Three targets is the maximum I'd put in a single episode, and it works here because the three are structurally interlocked. Kai's relevance failure creates the space for Maya's sufficiency failure (the compromise exists because Kai stripped out the educational question, so Maya's argument doesn't need substance — it just needs to overlap with Kai's logistics). Priya's source_credibility failure is the defensive response to the Maya-Kai coalition (she forms her own bloc to avoid being outnumbered). The three targets are not independent failures happening in the same room; they are a causal chain driven by the social dynamics of alliance formation.

The decision to put `source_credibility` on Priya without a cognitive_pattern target is deliberate. Priya's failure in this episode is primarily social positioning, not cognitive error. She is not reasoning poorly about the museum — she is *framing* the museum relative to the amusement park in a way that treats institutional prestige as evidence. The source_credibility weakness is in the framing ("real educational institution" as a quality claim rather than a category claim), and the authority_deference dynamic is the mechanism. Adding a cognitive_pattern would over-determine the target and make Priya look more flawed than the design doc intends in her pre-growth episodes. She is supposed to be competent and articulate, with a specific blind spot — not generally weak.

Maya's sufficiency target in this episode is deliberately distinct from her sufficiency target in episode 2. Episode 2's sufficiency was about *evidence* (one YouTube video). Episode 3's sufficiency is about *premises* (the argument's logical structure has thinned). This distinction creates a discrimination surface across episodes: a student tracking Maya's pattern should see that her arguments get weaker as the alliances get stronger, and the specific way they get weaker changes from "not enough evidence" to "not enough logical substance." The v2 analyst should be able to name both.

Sam carries two strengths in this episode. The condition_sensitivity question is her design-doc-described secondary strength — the one episode where she asks "under what conditions would X actually be true?" rather than her stable "does this answer the actual question?" (relevance). Pairing it with Kai's consequence_consideration strength means the episode has two characters asking good questions that the committee ignores — but for different reasons and from different angles. Sam's question is conceptual (what would have to be true?); Kai's is practical (what would actually happen?). Both are dismissed, but the dismissal mechanisms are different: Sam's question is too abstract for the committee's mood, and Kai's number is too concrete for anyone to want to engage with.

Kai's cost calculation is the single most important plant in the episode. The number on the whiteboard — $18 per student plus transport — is what triggers Priya's inflection in episode 4. It has to land here without being processed, so that when it returns in episode 4 it has the force of something the committee already knew and chose not to deal with. The closing beat (Sam looking at the whiteboard where her crossed-out proposal sits next to Kai's cost calculation) is the visual that encodes the episode's thesis: the right question and the hard number are both on the board, and the committee has organized itself into blocs that cannot see either one.

## Why these targets

**relevance / Kai / tunnel_vision / conflict_avoidance.** The design doc commits Kai to treating practical constraints as if they were the question, and to suppressing productive disagreement through impatience rather than fear. Episode 3 is where both surface most clearly because the alliance with Maya gives Kai's pragmatic frame a partner: logistics become the whole conversation because Kai and Maya have agreed to make them the whole conversation. tunnel_vision is the right pattern because Kai's frame is not wrong (logistics matter) — it is *narrow* (logistics are not the question the committee was asked). conflict_avoidance is the right dynamic because Kai's "let's move on" is not social cowardice; it is the pragmatist's instinct to suppress unresolvable argument, which in this case suppresses the one question that could resolve it.

**source_credibility / Priya / authority_deference.** This is the third variation of authority_deference across three episodes (Priya's personal version in episode 1, Jordan's credential version in episode 2, Priya's institutional-prestige version in episode 3). The repetition is pedagogically deliberate: authority_deference is not one thing, and students need to see its different faces. Priya's episode 3 version is the most abstract — "real educational institution" as a prestige category — and the most socially motivated (she is forming a bloc, not making an argument). No cognitive_pattern because the failure is social positioning, not cognitive error, and over-determining Priya's weaknesses before her growth arc would undermine the arc.

**sufficiency / Maya / confirmation_bias / group_pressure.** Maya's argument has thinned from episode 1 to episode 3, and tracking that thinning is one of the story's designed discrimination surfaces. Episode 1: inferential_validity (chain with gaps). Episode 2: sufficiency of evidence (one video). Episode 3: sufficiency of premises (the argument's logical structure has fewer claims). confirmation_bias persists because the underlying mechanism is stable: Maya filters toward her preferred conclusion. group_pressure returns because the bloc effect — two people agreeing treated as evidence — is the social-dynamics lesson of this episode. The coalition's size is doing the work the argument no longer does.

**condition_sensitivity (strength) / Sam.** The design doc names this as Sam's one-episode secondary strength. Episode 3 is the right placement because the alliances have made the committee's positions maximally rigid, which makes Sam's conditional reframe maximally useful and maximally unlikely to be heard. The question she asks ("under what conditions would the amusement park actually teach us something?") is a structural gift: it names the exact conditions (worksheet, physics teacher, measurement time, physics curriculum) that Maya's argument requires but has never specified. Its dismissal is the episode's sharpest moment of lost opportunity.

**consequence_consideration (strength) / Kai.** Kai's stable strength surfaces when he does cost math on the whiteboard. The number plants the seed for episode 4's inflection. Putting it here rather than episode 4 gives the number time to sit — the audience registers it in episode 3, and when it recurs in episode 4 with the free-lunch detail attached, the impact is compounded by recognition ("they already knew this").

## Authorial notes

Episode 2 shifts the primary lens from Logic to Evidence. Episode 1 was about the *structure* of the arguments (backward-built chains); episode 2 is about the *evidence* brought to support them. The distinction is pedagogically important: a student watching the series should feel the difference between "the argument doesn't hold together" (episode 1) and "the evidence doesn't hold up" (episode 2), even though both are failures of reasoning.

The stalemate shape is the right choice because the episode's dramatic function is stasis disguised as progress. Every committee member did homework. Every committee member brought something. The meeting feels productive — they have a brochure, a video, a printout, a map, a list of questions. But nobody changed their mind, nobody engaged with anyone else's evidence, and nobody asked the question that would have reframed the discussion: "are we evaluating destinations or answering a question?" The stalemate is what motivated reasoning looks like when everyone does it simultaneously — each person's evidence confirms their own position, so the group feels like it's moving forward while standing still.

Jordan becomes a lead in this episode because his source_credibility weakness is the cleanest instance of the evidence-lens problem. The zoo brochure is a perfect pedagogical object: every claim in it is true, the source is the institution being evaluated, and the credentials are real but irrelevant to the committee's question. A 6th grader reading this episode has to grapple with the difference between "is this source lying?" (no) and "is this source the right one to answer this question?" (also no — the zoo wrote the brochure to attract school groups, not to help a committee evaluate educational fit). The discrimination surface is rich: a student might flag source_credibility as "the brochure is biased" when the more precise observation is that the brochure is *promotional* — it is not unreliable, it is *the wrong kind of source* for this purpose.

Maya's target shifts from inferential_validity (episode 1) to sufficiency (episode 2). This is deliberate sequencing for the v2 assistive package's discrimination surfaces. Both are Logic-adjacent weaknesses that involve Maya's confident presentation, but they are different failures: a chain with gaps versus a chain with insufficient premises. The YouTube video is one data point presented as enough — that is sufficiency, not inferential_validity. A student who flags the same weakness code in both episodes is making a reasonable error that the assistive package should help them refine.

Maya's strength — catching the gap in Jordan's reasoning — is structurally important for two reasons. First, it establishes her design-doc-described dual nature: she is as good at finding gaps in others' reasoning as at hiding her own. Second, it creates a discrimination surface within a single character: the same cognitive faculty (chain analysis) is a strength when turned outward and a weakness when turned inward. This is the kind of complexity the v2 analyst agent should be able to name.

Sam's continued relevance strength is structurally the same as episode 1 but serves a different function in the stalemate shape. In episode 1's early_strength_collapse, Sam's strength surfaced and was buried by social momentum. In episode 2's stalemate, Sam's strength is present but structurally invisible — her questions are not formatted as arguments, so the committee literally does not know how to engage with them. The failure mode is different: episode 1's failure was social (the room's energy buried her), episode 2's failure is structural (the committee's implicit rules exclude her contribution). This distinction matters for the continuation chain: a student who notices "Sam's questions are the right kind of questions but the committee treats them as off-topic" has identified a relevance failure in the group's process, not just a social dynamic.

Priya is present but not a lead. Her museum printout is mentioned to keep her proposal visible, but the episode does not focus on her because the ironic perspective_engagement weakness from episode 1 needs to simmer before it becomes a target again. She is competent and polished in this episode, which is the necessary pre-growth baseline — students need to see Priya being effective before they see her being blind.

## Why these targets

**source_credibility / Jordan / uncritical_acceptance / authority_deference.** The design doc commits Jordan to evidence-leaning reasoning that trusts official sources without interrogation. Episode 2 is the establishing instance because the Evidence lens makes Jordan's pattern most visible — this is the episode where what counts as evidence is the central question, and Jordan's answer ("official credentials count") is the cleanest wrong answer. The zoo brochure is the ideal vehicle: true, promotional, credentialed, and irrelevant to the committee's actual question. uncritical_acceptance is the right cognitive pattern because Jordan is not filtering evidence toward a conclusion (that would be confirmation_bias) — he is accepting a source at face value because it looks authoritative. authority_deference is the social dynamic because the committee follows his lead in treating credentials as conclusive, extending the deference from the credential to Jordan's reading of it.

**sufficiency / Maya / confirmation_bias.** Maya's episode 2 target must be different from her episode 1 target to establish that her pattern has multiple expressions. The YouTube video is one piece of evidence presented as sufficient — a different failure mode than the inferential gap in her episode 1 chain. confirmation_bias is the correct pattern for both episodes because the underlying mechanism is the same (Maya filters toward her preferred conclusion) even though the surface expression is different (episode 1: structural leaps; episode 2: evidence thinness). No social dynamic target on this entry because the social pressure Maya exerts is already established in episode 1 and will return sharply in episode 3's alliance formation; episode 2 isolates the cognitive dimension.

**inferential_validity (strength) / Maya.** The design doc establishes Maya's dual nature: chain-building skill applied outward is a strength, applied inward is a weakness. Episode 2 is the right place to show the outward application because the stalemate shape means no one fully wins — Maya's observation about Jordan's credentials is correct and lands, but it does not change the committee's trajectory. The selective application (she sees Jordan's gap but not her own equivalent gap) is the richest single beat for the v2 assistive package's counterfactual analysis: "Maya could have asked herself the same question she asked Jordan."

**relevance (strength) / Sam.** Sam carries relevance as a stable strength across the season. Episode 2 re-establishes it in a different failure mode (structural invisibility vs. episode 1's social burial) to show that the committee's inability to hear Sam is not a one-time event but a systemic feature of how they have framed the task.

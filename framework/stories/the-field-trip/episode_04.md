## Authorial notes

This is the episode the story has been building toward. Every plant from episodes 1–3 pays off here: Kai's cost number from the whiteboard, Sam's unanswered questions from episode 2, Priya's unexamined position from episode 1. The episode's job is to make one character see what she could not see before, and to make that recognition feel earned rather than imposed.

The self_correction shape is the only shape that works for a growth-arc inflection. The correction is Priya's — she catches her own blind spot without anyone accusing her of having one. The mechanism is Sam's quiet factual question ("have you checked if they cover field trips?"), which is devastating precisely because it is not an attack. Sam is not saying "you're privileged" or "you don't understand." She is asking a factual question that Priya cannot answer, and Priya's inability to answer it tells her everything she needs to know about why she never asked it.

The decision to put only two targets in this episode (down from three in episode 3) is deliberate. The inflection needs room to breathe. Priya's perspective_engagement target is the episode's center of gravity, and Kai's reasoning_completeness target is the counterweight — Kai's reductive response to the equity question ("so we go with the cheaper option") shows that recognizing a problem and understanding a problem are different things. Two targets, clearly sequenced, with the inflection between them.

Priya's target has no social_dynamic because the inflection is private. This is load-bearing for the growth arc: if Priya's recognition were driven by social pressure (the group shaming her into recanting), the growth would be performative, not genuine. The design doc specifies that Priya's growth is real but uncomfortable — she has to sit with having been the scope-blind character for three episodes. Making the inflection cognitive rather than social is what makes it honest. She is not apologizing to the group; she is confronting herself.

Kai's reasoning_completeness target introduces a new facet to the story. This is the right episode for it because the equity question is genuinely multi-dimensional — cost, access, fairness, educational purpose — and Kai's pragmatic frame can only hold one dimension at a time. The distinction from episode 3's relevance target matters: in episode 3, Kai's logistics were *irrelevant* to the educational question (he was answering the wrong question). In episode 4, Kai's cost focus is *relevant but incomplete* (he is answering part of the right question). The v2 assistive package should be able to name this progression.

Sam carries three strengths in this episode, which is unusual but earned. Her perspective_engagement strength is the new one — she has carried relevance and condition_sensitivity before, but this is the first time she adds a scope dimension by naming whose experience is missing. The perspective_engagement strength is what makes Priya's inflection possible: Sam provides the specific input (six students on free lunch, have you checked the scholarships?) that Priya's self_correction requires. Her relevance strength recurs in a new form — her proposal resurfaces not because she pushes it but because the cost conversation has changed what "relevant" means to the committee. And Kai's consequence_consideration strength recurs because his cost arithmetic is again the concrete anchor that makes the abstract discussion land.

The closing beat — Sam's proposal uncrossed on the whiteboard, the committee uncertain about what to recommend — sets up episode 5's messy final debate. The committee has been shaken but not reorganized. The blocs from episode 3 have not cleanly dissolved; Maya is still arguing for the amusement park (she is not a lead in this episode and her position has not been directly challenged by the cost conversation). Jordan is still holding the zoo (same). What has changed is the emotional ground: Priya's withdrawal removes one pillar of the "serious institution" bloc, and Sam's proposal is no longer invisible. Episode 5 inherits a committee that is more honest than it was but not yet honest enough.

The moment of surprise (rubric item 9) is in this episode. It is Priya's silence after Sam's question. The surprise is not that Priya is wrong — the reader has seen her blind spot since episode 1. The surprise is the *quality* of her recognition: she does not argue, deflect, or perform contrition. She goes quiet. For a character whose defining trait is articulate confidence, silence is the most surprising and most in-character response possible. She does not know what to say because she has never been in a position where she did not know what to say. That is the moment.

## Why these targets

**perspective_engagement / Priya / egocentric_thinking.** This is the climax of Priya's growth arc. The design doc establishes that perspective_engagement is both her ironic weakness (episodes 1–3: the faculty she is known for fails on the case where the perspective she needs to take is her own) and her eventual genuine strength (episode 5: post-growth, the question is honest). Episode 4 is the inflection between those two states. egocentric_thinking is the right cognitive pattern because the failure is positional: Priya's inability to see the cost question is not intellectual laziness — it is the specific blindness of someone who has never needed to think about affordability. No social_dynamic because the inflection must be self-generated, not socially coerced. The self_correction shape requires Priya to catch herself, which she does — and the catch is what makes her episode 5 perspective_engagement genuine rather than performative.

**reasoning_completeness / Kai / tunnel_vision.** Kai's second target introduces a new facet while maintaining his stable cognitive pattern. The design doc describes Kai as someone whose pragmatic frame crowds out the question being asked; in episode 3 the crowding was total (logistics replaced the educational question), while in episode 4 the crowding is partial (cost is relevant but not sufficient). reasoning_completeness is the right facet because Kai's reasoning is not wrong — cost does matter — it is just missing dimensions. tunnel_vision persists because the mechanism is the same: Kai's pragmatic frame can hold one variable at a time. No social_dynamic because Kai's reductive response ("so we go with the cheaper option") is a cognitive reflex, not a social move — he is not trying to shut down the conversation, he is genuinely trying to solve the problem with the tool he has.

**perspective_engagement (strength) / Sam.** Sam's new strength is the episode's catalyst. Her factual question about the free-lunch students is the most important line in the story — it is what makes the cost number personal rather than logistical, and it is what triggers Priya's self_correction. Pairing perspective_engagement-as-strength on Sam with perspective_engagement-as-weakness on Priya in the same episode creates the sharpest possible contrast: the quiet kid who has been dismissed for four episodes provides the specific insight that the articulate kid could not see. The v2 assistive package should find this pairing rich — the same facet, opposite valences, same episode, and the weakness-carrier's recognition of the strength-carrier's point is the story's turning point.

**consequence_consideration (strength) / Kai, relevance (strength) / Sam.** Both recur from earlier episodes. Kai's cost arithmetic is the concrete anchor (same function as episode 3, now with the free-lunch detail attached). Sam's relevance strength returns in its most effective form — her proposal is no longer invisible because the committee's frame has shifted enough to see it. Neither strength is new; both land differently because the context has changed.

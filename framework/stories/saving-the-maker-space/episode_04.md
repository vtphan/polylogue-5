## Authorial notes

This is the inflection episode for the season's major growth arc. It has to land Sam's intervention in a way that feels both inevitable (episodes 1 through 3 have been setting up her silence as a structural feature of the group, not a personality quirk) and fragile (she is not becoming a confident person; she is doing one hard thing once and it costs her). Both halves matter. If Sam's intervention reads as a switch flipping, the growth arc is dishonest — the design doc explicitly commits her to ending the season as "a kid who *can* speak up when the stakes are high enough, not as a kid who reliably does."

The structural challenge is the double target on perspective_engagement — Mira as weakness carrier in the opening, Sam as both weakness and strength carrier across the same episode. story_consistency_reviewer needs to read this as the inflection rather than as a contradiction. The way to make that legible is to keep the timing of the beats unmistakable: Sam's silence is the weakness in beats 1–4, Sam's intervention is the strength in beats 5–7, and the same beat 5 ("Does it?") is the hinge. The weakness target's interaction_note flags this explicitly so the reviewer doesn't have to infer it.

Ren's role in this episode is to do two things: to carry the smaller growth arc (the audit of her own sources from episode 1 finally happening, between meetings) and to be the one who gives Sam social cover after the intervention. The order matters — Ren reports her audit *before* Sam intervenes, so that by the time Sam speaks the room has already been told that one piece of episode 1 was wrong. Sam's intervention then lands as a second correction, not a first one, and the audience reads it as the same kind of move as Ren's audit rather than as a thunderbolt. This is a deliberate creative choice: making Sam's intervention dramatic enough to register but not so dramatic that it breaks her character.

Mira's behavior in this episode is the most delicate of the season for her. She has to be visibly working — she is drafting a second petition; she is taking the principal's pushback seriously (the new draft has a different evidence base) — and she still has to carry the perspective failure unselfconsciously. The line "we know what the kids need" has to come out of her mouth in the opening beats *without* her hearing what's wrong with it, because the whole point of the episode is that the group's perspective failure has been invisible to the people inside it. Her catching herself at beat 8 (opening her mouth to restate the consensus and stopping) is the first time she registers that the move she has been making for three episodes is the move that needs to stop.

Theo and Dev are not leads in this episode and should appear only briefly. Theo's intervention in episode 3 has done its work; episode 4 is not about him recovering. Dev is in the room but not speaking — episode 5 brings him back. Their presence has to be felt without taking screen time from Sam.

The closing beat ("Sam walking out of the maker space at the end of the meeting, looking unsteady") is critical and the dialog writer has to land it without narration. Sam has done the hardest thing she has ever done in this group, and the audience needs to see that it took something from her. The growth is real *and* it is costly *and* episode 5 is not going to redeem the cost — that is the deliberate refusal of tidy resolution at the character level, mirroring the season's refusal at the plot level.

## Why these targets

**perspective_engagement / Mira / group_pressure.** Mira has to carry the perspective failure in the opening because Mira drafted the petition and the petition is the artifact whose perspective is being audited. The lens is scope (perspective_engagement is structurally a scope-lens facet). I left the cognitive_pattern null deliberately — Mira's false_certainty is active in the prose but the *engineered* failure here is perspective, not certainty, and double-loading would obscure which failure Sam's intervention corrects. The social signal is the fourth iteration of Mira's premature-consensus move, and the iteration matters: episode 4 is the episode where the move breaks for the second time (Theo broke it for the first time in episode 3), this time by being broken from a direction nobody in the room expected.

**perspective_engagement / Sam / conflict_avoidance.** This is the only episode in the season where Sam carries a target, and she has to carry the same facet she will then strengthen, because the inflection IS the carrier flipping valence on the same facet within the same episode. The conflict_avoidance social_dynamic attaches to Sam here for the same structural reason — it has been her stable mode for three episodes, and episode 4 is when it gets broken. Putting both her weakness and her strength on the same facet is unusual but doctrinally sound: the inflection is what carrying the same facet in two valences in one episode *means*. story_consistency_reviewer's job is to read this as growth, not as contradiction; the interaction_note tells the reviewer how to read it.

**perspective_engagement (strength) / Sam.** The strength has to be Sam, the facet has to be perspective_engagement, and the episode has to be 4. There is no other configuration that could land the season's major growth arc. The note in the strengths entry calls out the specific line ("the petition was written for the forty") because story_consistency_reviewer needs to be able to check the eventual transcript against a specific target sentence, not just a general intent.

**source_credibility (strength) / Ren.** Ren's growth arc inflection lives in this episode because her arc is the smaller of the two and it has to land in the same episode as Sam's, otherwise it gets crowded out. The note calls out that her audit happens *between meetings* — that is the point of her growth, that she is now doing the work between sessions instead of bringing whatever she found at random. The "two of them aren't actually about us" line is the specific dialog beat the reviewer can check.

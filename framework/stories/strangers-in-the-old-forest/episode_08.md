## Authorial notes

Episode 8 is the evidence arc's payoff episode and Cam's inflection.
It is structurally the busiest episode in the season so far — one
weakness target, three strength targets, two of them on the same
character, a social-dynamic reversal, Jules's acceptance-not-growth
move, and a regrouping closing beat. The density is load-bearing
and should not be trimmed. What keeps the scene from feeling
crowded is that most of the moves are short, flat, and delivered
without affect: Cam's retelling is matter-of-fact, Priya's naming
is one sentence, Jules's acceptance is quiet, the closing regroup
is practical rather than triumphant. The dialog writer should treat
*low-affect delivery of high-stakes content* as the scene's central
register.

The call to Anya itself is off-screen and is not staged. What the
audience gets is Cam's retelling at the picnic table. This is
deliberate — the whole arc of Anya in this season is *reported
speech inside the kids' turns*, and ep 8's delta from ep 2 is
specifically in the *difference between two kids' retellings* of
two different calls. Ep 2 Jules reported what Anya said with the
caveats softened and the framing promoted. Ep 8 Cam reports what
Anya said having asked different questions and carrying back
different summaries. The reader is meant to feel the delta and,
through it, see confirmation bias as something that lives in *which
questions a listener asks*, not in *what the speaker said*. The
design doc is explicit that this is the structural move letting
6th-graders see confirmation bias without needing to hear Anya
directly.

On Cam's two strengths in one episode: this is the design. The
arc summary explicitly locks both moves in the same scene and
names the reason: *that self-audit is the confirmation that his
growth is real and not externally driven.* Splitting the two
strengths into two facets (source_credibility for the Anya call;
source_diversity for the self-audit) is the cleanest coverage —
both facets were Cam's weaknesses earlier in the season (ep 1/2
source_credibility; ep 3 source_diversity) and both reverse here.
`sufficiency`, his third declared weakness facet, is not reversed
in ep 8 and lands its strength in ep 10 per the design-doc pattern
(weakness episodes 1 through 3, strength episodes 8 and 10).

On Cam calling Anya without prompting: the arc summary is explicit
— *he calls Anya himself, unprompted by any adult.* The dialog
writer should stage the call as already-done by the time the scene
opens. Cam does not ask the group's permission to have called; he
does not apologize for having called without telling them. He just
reports. The move being *already done* is part of what makes it a
growth beat — the provenance habit has become something he reaches
for on his own, not something someone else has to nudge him into.

On Maya's weakness landing on `reasoning_completeness`, not
`inferential_validity`: the shape is that Maya *can feel* the
chain has one more sentence in it (*and the framing you brought
home was a side-project hypothesis, not a mainline finding*) and
stops before saying it because Jules is fragile. That is a
completeness failure — the chain she would have produced would
have closed, and she does not produce it. This is the same shape
as ep 2's half-objection (*wait, how did your brother know she was
the right person to —*) where Maya also stopped mid-sentence because
of Jules's fragility, and the two episodes are meant to read as
the same move in the same pattern: the friend-protective form of
group_pressure producing an incomplete chain. The story's arc
*reverses* this pattern here not by having Maya push through the
chain (which the design doc flags as ep 10 territory, not ep 8),
but by having Priya *name* the pressure so Maya can see the
constraint she was placing on herself. Ep 8 is the naming episode;
ep 10 is where Maya has internalized the reversal and no longer
needs to be named to.

On Priya's strength being `perspective_engagement` rather than
something group-dynamic-specific: the declared facet inventory
does not include a social-dynamic-naming facet, and
perspective_engagement is the closest structural fit. Priya is
reframing the *frame* Maya has been operating in — from *soften or
hurt Jules* to *caring for Jules and finishing the chain are not
in conflict* — and reframing-the-frame is the signature
perspective_engagement move. The story's scope arc trains Priya to
ask *what is missing from the current frame*, and in ep 8 she is
applying that same habit to the social dimension of the scene
rather than the data-collection dimension. The continuity between
her ep-7 habitat-island observation and her ep-8 friend-pressure
naming is exactly this: both are *the frame you are inside is not
the only frame* moves, one on physical data, one on social caring.

On Jules without a target in ep 8: Jules has no growth arc, and
her accepting Cam's report quietly is engine-not-growth. Putting
a weakness target on her in ep 8 would force a pattern-surface
where the scene needs stillness — the emotional center of the
episode is Jules's rattling being handled with care, and a
designed move on Jules would compete with that. Her patterns from
prior episodes (`false_cause`, `black_and_white_thinking`,
`authority_deference`) are present as residue in her visible
rattling — these are the patterns the authority-revelation is
undercutting — but the surfacing of the residue is emotional
rather than cognitive, and should stay at the beat level. The
story's final Jules move (ep 10's noticing Anya's defensiveness
on the third call and not pressing) is engine-not-growth at scale,
and ep 8 is the setup.

On the social dynamic pair (Maya group_pressure weakness + Priya
perspective_engagement strength): the two moves must stage as one
turn-pair shape across multiple turns. The dialog writer should
treat them as connected — Maya softens, then (one or two turns
later) Priya names it, then Maya's face changes. The texture is a
*quiet reframe*, not a *public correction*. Priya is not telling
Maya she did the wrong thing; she is offering a frame that makes
Maya's constraint visible to Maya. That distinction is load-bearing
for how the transcript reader experiences Priya — she is not the
scold of the group; she is the person who notices what the frame
is hiding.

## Why these targets

**reasoning_completeness / Maya / group_pressure.** The design doc
names ep 8 as the episode where the friend-protective
`group_pressure` dynamic returns and gets named by Priya. Maya is
the natural carrier because this is her ep-2 pattern in the same
shape — she can feel the chain has one more sentence in it and
stops because the next sentence would hurt Jules. The facet is
`reasoning_completeness` (chain-not-finishing, same as ep 2, not
the `inferential_validity` of ep 4's chain-not-building), and the
social_dynamic is `group_pressure` in its friend-protective form
(not peer-conformity form). The design doc is explicit that
reversing this pattern is the arc of episodes 5 and 8 — ep 5
partially reversed it by Maya completing a chain without Jules
present to trigger the pressure; ep 8 tests the reversal with Jules
present and rattled, and Maya almost-reverses but slips back into
the softening. Priya's naming of the pressure is the part of the
reversal that lands in ep 8; Maya's internalization of the
reversal is ep 10 territory.

**source_credibility (strength) / Cam.** The design doc commits
Cam's inflection to this facet in ep 8 and specifies the move — he
calls Anya himself, asks plain provenance questions, reports the
answers without editorial. This is the *credentialed* half of
Cam's source-audit learning (the creator-online half was seeded in
ep 2 and practiced in ep 5). The facet choice is fixed by the
arc and by the ep 1/2 pairing: source_credibility was Cam's
primary weakness facet in those episodes, and ep 8 is the reversal
at full scale.

**source_diversity (strength) / Cam.** The design doc commits the
ep-3 post self-audit to ep 8 and flags it as the *confirmation
that Cam's growth is real and not externally driven*. The facet
is source_diversity because the specific blind spot Cam recognizes
is that the replies to his post were not independent sources —
they were downstream responses to his own framing. This is a
direct reversal of the ep-3 weakness on the same facet, and the
arc benefits from both reversals (source_credibility and
source_diversity) landing in the same episode to make the
arc-level move feel unified.

**perspective_engagement (strength) / Priya.** The design doc
describes the ep-8 friend-pressure naming as Priya's move and
quotes the line directly (*we can be nice to Jules and still say
what we found*). The facet is perspective_engagement because the
move is a frame-widening move — reframing Maya's binary (soften
or hurt Jules) into a frame where both values can be held at once.
This is Priya's second perspective_engagement beat (ep 7 planted
the facet on a physical-data frame; ep 8 applies it on a social
frame), and the two beats together establish the facet as her
growing strength for the back half of the season.

## Calibration note for this episode

The emotional register is *low affect, high content*. Every move
in this scene is delivered flat — Cam's retelling is matter-of-
fact, Cam's self-audit is small and a little proud, Jules's
quiet processing is quiet, Priya's naming of the pressure is
plain, Jules's final acceptance is small. None of these beats
should be staged as dramatic. The dialog writer should resist any
temptation to make Cam sound triumphant about having called Anya,
to make Jules sound devastated about Anya-being-a-physicist, to
make Priya sound admonishing when she names the pressure, or to
make Maya sound defensive when she softens the landing. The whole
scene is a group of 11-year-olds doing hard adult-ish things
without anyone dramatizing that they are doing them. The
uncanny-not-horror-adjacent calibration is basically dormant here
— the fear has dissolved enough across ep 5, 6, 7 that ep 8 is
about *processing information*, not about *being scared*. Jules's
rattling should read as *disappointment and recalibration*, not
*betrayal*. The friendship is the room and the room is steady.
The scene should feel, at the end, like four kids who have done
their work and know it — not proud, not tired, just a little more
sure of the ground than they were this morning.

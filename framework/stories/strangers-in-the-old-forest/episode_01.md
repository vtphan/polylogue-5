## Authorial notes

This is the establishing episode and it has to plant every major thing
the season will spend nine more episodes working through, without
letting any one of those plants read as a *lesson*. The four kids are
being themselves at the picnic table, and the four versions of being
themselves happen to contain the weak moves the story needs. That is
the register — *these are 11-year-olds, not illustrations*.

The `early_strength_collapse` shape runs through the scene's social
architecture, not through a dramatic beat. Maya's strength is real —
she is the only person who names what the group is actually trying to
answer — and the collapse is the absence of engagement. It is not that
the other three reject her question; it is that Jules's emotional
momentum, Cam's casual citing, and Priya's easy generalizing are all
more socially engaging than opening a written question is. The reader
should feel the collapse as structural: Maya did the most load-bearing
thing in the scene and the scene did not reward her for it. That
pattern reversing — the group starting to engage with what Maya writes
— is the arc of episodes 5, 9, and 10.

Evidence is the primary lens because this episode's central failure is
the quality of what the four of them are treating as *evidence*. Jules
is running a causal story off one observation. Cam is putting a TikTok
next to a memory next to a kid's guess as if the three belong on the
same table. The episode does not need to be about *sources* in a
pedagogical sense; it needs to be about four kids talking confidently
at a picnic table, and the weak evidence moves should feel like
natural 6th-grade talking, not like illustrations of a fallacy. That
naturalness is exactly what makes the moves hard for a student to spot
on first reading, which is what makes the episode a good first pass of
the Evidence lens.

On Jules: her signals here are `false_cause` on a base of one
observation, producing the season's purest `sufficiency` weakness. I
deliberately held `black_and_white_thinking` back to episode 2. The
aliens-or-boring framing that `black_and_white_thinking` names needs
Anya's voice in the room (reported speech) to land cleanly — without
it, Jules's fear in episode 1 reads as the *origin* of the investigation,
which it is, rather than as a dichotomy she has collapsed her thinking
into, which it becomes after Anya. One cognitive pattern in episode 1,
two starting in episode 2.

On Priya: her weakness in this episode is facet-level with no named
cognitive pattern, which is deliberate and mirrors the design doc. The
manifestation — *squirrels don't have red eyes anywhere I've been* —
is a sentence that reads as observation and lands as conclusion, and
that is exactly the subtlety the scope lens teaches students to catch.
Forcing a cognitive_pattern onto this target (e.g. `overgeneralization`)
would sharpen the signal in the wrong direction: Priya is not
overgeneralizing from a lot of data to a bigger claim, she is drawing
from the *conditions under which she has walked*. That is a scope
move, not a logic move. The signal therefore lives in the beats and
the transcript behavior, not in a cognitive_signal field. Episode 4 is
where her condition_sensitivity weakness surfaces in its cleanest
single-sentence form; episode 1 is the foreshadow.

On Cam: `source_credibility` rather than `source_diversity` because the
episode-1 version of Cam's move is treating *one* TikTok as if it is
information, not mixing many sources into one low-quality pile. The
diversity weakness shows up more cleanly in episode 3 when his post
draws a handful of replies and he treats that handful as a set. Here
the move is: this TikTok is evidence because it exists and I saw it,
and its maker is someone the group is implicitly asked to trust because
Cam is passing it along. The `uncritical_acceptance` is Cam's — he
watched, he accepted, he's relaying — and the lens is Evidence.

On Maya: `reasoning_completeness` as the seed strength because her
move is naming the open question — *what are we actually trying to
figure out* — and leaving room for the investigation to return to it.
`internal_consistency` was the alternative but fits episode 5 better,
when Cam reads an earlier sentence back to her and she revises on the
page. Completeness here is about the *question* staying in view; ep 5
is about the *claim* staying in view. Different moves. Both are
Maya's. Episode 1 gets the question-shaped one.

No social dynamics this episode. The arc summary places the first
`group_pressure` beat (friend-protective form) in episode 2, where
Jules is visibly scared and Maya softens her pushback. `authority_deference`
is planted in episode 2 via the Anya retelling. Episode 1 is cognitive
patterns and facet-level moves only — which is the right shape for an
establishing episode, because introducing four characters, the
premise, the park, and the mystery is already a full plate.

## Why these targets

**sufficiency / Jules / false_cause.** The design doc commits Jules to
`sufficiency` and `inferential_validity` across the season, and
names episode 1 specifically as "the season's purest `sufficiency`
weakness." One observation extended into a causal story is exactly
that: the base is thin, the chain is tight, and the gap is quantity,
not logic. Pairing `false_cause` as the cognitive pattern makes the
signal concrete — the specific shape of "something changed them →
something must have caused the change" is the fallacy the chain is
running on. This plants the pattern cleanly for the audience and sets
up episode 2, where the same pattern will be reinforced by Anya's
framing and get a social dynamic (`authority_deference`) layered on
top.

**source_credibility / Cam / uncritical_acceptance.** The design doc
establishes Cam's primary weakness as `uncritical_acceptance` and
describes his episode-1 move as citing a TikTok as if it is evidence.
The facet is `source_credibility` because the move is about *trusting a
specific source without asking who made it*. This is the seed for
Cam's whole arc — his episode-8 inflection is exactly the reverse move
(he calls Anya himself and asks plainly what her dissertation is on),
and for that inflection to land, the starting position has to be
clearly visible in episode 1. The TikTok is the easiest, most honest
version of the move for a kid in 2026 whose feed is his information
environment.

**condition_sensitivity / Priya / (facet-level).** The design doc is
explicit that Priya's weakness is facet-level with no named cognitive
pattern; her weak move in episode 1 is the seed for the
condition_sensitivity weakness that fully plants in episode 4 and
inflects to strength in episode 7. Having her weak move show up in
episode 1 in a soft, easy-to-miss form — the *squirrels don't have red
eyes anywhere I've been* sentence — lets the planning_agent translate
it into a subtle transcript beat that students can catch on re-reading
once episode 4 has sharpened what to look for. Episode 1 does not need
to teach the scope lens; it needs to plant Priya's habit.

**reasoning_completeness (strength) / Maya.** Maya's season arc moves
from feel-first reasoning to formal chain-writing across episodes 4,
5, 9, and 10. Episode 1 plants a proto-move: she writes the question
down so the group can come back to it. This is the seed version of
what she does at scale in episode 9, when she builds the full genetic
chain. The early-strength-collapse shape requires the strength to be
recognizable and unrecognized: Maya makes the right move, the group
does not engage, and the notebook closes with the question still on
the page. That collapse is what the ep-5 peer-audit moment reverses.
The reader should feel, by episode 5, that the kind of thing Maya
wrote in episode 1 is *finally* getting engaged with.

## Calibration note for this episode

This is the first scene students will read. Jules's fear is present
but has not yet surfaced as fear — it is underneath her retelling, in
the urgency and the repetition, not in the words. The dialog writer
should treat the fear as *uncanny, not horror-adjacent* (per the story
design doc's calibration warning): Jules is unsettled, not afraid of
being hurt. Adults are absent from this scene entirely, not hostile or
menacing. The friendship is the room — four kids who have been the
four of them since 4th grade, who meet here every week — and the
weight of the scene is the strangeness of their park, not the threat
of a predator or a force. Episode 1 sets the emotional thermostat for
the season; *unsettled but together* is the right reading.

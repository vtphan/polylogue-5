## Authorial notes

Episode 10 is the season finale and the story's structural
resolution. It is the episode in which the work gets *finished*
rather than the episode in which the answer gets *found* — the
answer landed in ep 9, and ep 10 is about putting the notebook
down. The register the dialog writer must hold across the full
scene is *relief, not triumph*. Nothing in this episode is staged
as climax. The chain closed yesterday; the correction post is
posted; Anya reframes and Jules notices; the notebook is closed.
The pedagogical commitment the design doc names — *nobody claims
certainty; the answer is right and the thread is thinner than
anyone would like, and saying so is the most grown-up move in the
story* — is the through-line. Every beat should be checked
against *does this sound triumphant?* and dialed back if yes.

On the single weakness target being on Jules: three structural
reasons. First, the design doc commits the Jules noticing-without-
pressing move to ep 10 as *the story's final word on what it
means to have outgrown an authority*, and framing it as an
authority_deference pull that Jules catches mid-move is the only
way to stage the outgrowing without turning Jules into a growth-
arc character (which the design doc explicitly refuses). Second,
authority_deference has never been a targeted weakness across the
season — it has been texture on Jules's retellings in eps 2 and 8
— and ep 10 is the last chance to land it on the one carrier who
can plausibly hold it. Third, staging it as a pull caught and not
as a pull averted is the honest picture: Jules's default is still
authority-deferential; six weeks of her friends' reasoning has
produced awareness of the default, not elimination of it. The
half-sentence stop — *well, I mean, maybe we're —* — is the
reversal, and the unsaid completion is what the ep-2 Jules would
have said.

On the facet pairing for Jules's target being source_credibility
rather than some other facet: the move Jules catches is Anya's
defensiveness reading as a credibility signal. In ep 2 Jules
deferred to Anya's credentials without asking who Anya actually
was; in ep 10 Anya's retreat from her earlier framing *is itself*
credibility information about Anya, and Jules's pull to soften
would have discarded that signal. The catch preserves it. The
symmetry with ep 2 is exact — same carrier, same facet, same
social dynamic, opposite outcome — which is the arc-level move
the design doc has been pointing at. No cognitive pattern is
targeted because authority_deference is a social dynamic, not a
cognitive one, and the design doc has never attached a named
cognitive pattern to Jules's deference move.

On the weakness staging as internal and unspoken: the dialog
writer is operating without access to the episode plan, so the
catch must be visible in dialogue alone. The half-sentence stop
is the primary signal — Jules starts a sentence and does not
finish it. The minimal closing of the call is the secondary
signal — *right. okay. thanks for your time.* — which reads as
reserve rather than warmth because the ep-2 Jules would have
been warmer with Anya. The group's silent registration after the
call is the tertiary signal — Maya writing something in the
notebook rather than speaking, a beat of quiet. The
transcript_reviewer should expect these three signals together
and should flag if the catch is verbalized (either by Jules or
by another character naming it).

On Maya's strength being internal_consistency rather than
another logic facet: internal_consistency is the last of Maya's
three declared Logic facets and the one that has not yet
received a targeted strength beat (ep 5 caught it on-page inside
a reasoning_completeness scene, but not as its own target). The
ep-10 move is different in kind from ep 5's catch: ep 5 was
drift caught by someone else reading back a sentence; ep 10 is
Maya reading her own earlier question back, by choice, as a
method move before closing the notebook. That distinction is
internal_consistency as formalized habit rather than as
in-motion catch, and it earns the facet as settled. The
design-doc end-state for Maya — *the kid who formalizes things
without being asked, because she has learned that her father's
rule — show your work in a sermon or nobody trusts you — applies
to her own thinking as well as to preaching* — is the scene's
pedagogical closure. By closing the notebook on a consistency
check rather than on the chain itself, Maya demonstrates that
the habit has become method.

On Cam's sufficiency strength being the final correction-post
draft rather than the first draft: the schema supports targeting
the strength specifically on the calibrated version, which is
the pedagogically load-bearing move. The first draft is staging
— real-time drafting is how a 6th-grader actually writes a
correction post, and showing the revision process is honest — but
the first draft is not itself a weakness target, because making
it one would crowd the episode past the design's committed
surface area and would dilute the closure Cam's arc needs. The
three-part structure of the final draft (what the chain
supports, what it does not, what would falsify it) is the
sufficiency move in its calibrated form: enough evidence for
what kind of claim, explicit about the scope of the claim. The
bonus observation — fewer replies than the original — stages as
small group texture without any character moralizing about it.
The learning is absorbed, not announced.

On Priya having no targeted beat in ep 10: her arc settled in
ep 9 and her ep-7 and ep-8 facets are already planted as
strengths. Carrying a third targeted strength on Priya here
would crowd the scene and would teach no new move. She remains
a lead character and contributes substantively — proposing the
Anya call, helping draft the falsifiability clause, helping Cam
calibrate the correction — but those contributions are
collaboration texture, not pedagogical target. This is the right
shape for the finale: Priya has finished her growth and is now
operating at settled capacity, the same way Maya will operate at
settled capacity in the episodes-that-do-not-exist-beyond-this-
one.

On the Anya call being the third and final reported-speech
encounter: the season's Anya arc is structurally three points
(ep 2 Jules-softened, ep 8 Cam-plain, ep 10 Anya-defensive), and
each of the three calls is encountered as *reported speech
inside the kids' turns*. Ep 10's call is on speakerphone and is
slightly different — the audience hears what Anya said more
directly through Jules's repetition rather than through summary.
That shift is deliberate: the reader needs to hear Anya's
goalpost move clearly enough to see that Jules's catch is a
response to a specific sentence, and speakerphone staging is the
cleanest way to make Anya's sentence visible without breaking
the cast rule that no adult ever speaks on-screen. The dialog
writer should stage Jules's relay of Anya's sentence as close to
verbatim as 6th-grade dialogue can carry — Jules repeats what
Anya said rather than summarizing it.

On the fear dissolving into relief-not-triumph: the design doc
is explicit that the season ends without a triumphant
announcement, and ep 10's closing beats should enforce this with
specific negative constraints. Nobody declares the mystery
solved. Nobody says *we were right*. Nobody says *see, Jules, it
wasn't aliens*. The notebook getting closed, the fewer-replies
observation landed flat, and the small half-sentence exchange
about how the Saturday felt are the closing register. The
friendship is the room and the room is steady. The scene should
end with the four of them sitting at the picnic table for a beat
longer than strictly necessary, and then the scene is over. No
resolution dialogue, no *so what did we learn*, no arc summary
spoken aloud by any character.

## Why these targets

**source_credibility / Jules / authority_deference.** The design
doc commits Jules's final move to ep 10 and frames it as outgrowing
an authority. The clean way to target that move is as a residual
authority_deference pull caught mid-move on a source_credibility
facet — symmetrical with ep 2 (same carrier, same facet, same
social dynamic, opposite outcome) and consistent with the design
doc's engine-not-growth principle (the pull is still her default;
awareness of it is what is new). No other character naturally
carries this move, no other episode is available to land
authority_deference as a targeted weakness, and the ep-2/ep-10
arc-level symmetry is the season's most structural move on this
facet.

**internal_consistency (strength) / Maya.** The design doc
commits Maya's arc to three episodes — ep 5, ep 9, ep 10 — and
her three declared Logic facets to those three episodes in some
order. Ep 5 planted reasoning_completeness; ep 9 planted
inferential_validity; ep 10 is the natural place for
internal_consistency, staged as Maya reading her ep-1 question
back and checking claim-now against claim-then as deliberate
method. The design doc's Maya end-state — *the kid who
formalizes things without being asked* — is earned on-screen by
this move. No other character naturally carries it, and no other
episode is available to land the facet.

**sufficiency (strength) / Cam.** The design doc commits
sufficiency to land as strength in ep 10 specifically, and names
the correction post as the natural staging. The three-part
calibrated draft (what the chain supports, what it does not,
what would falsify it) is the sufficiency move in its settled
form — enough evidence for what kind of claim, explicit about
the scope. This closes Cam's Evidence arc with the third of his
three declared facets landing as strength, in symmetry with
source_credibility (ep 8) and source_diversity (ep 8).

## Calibration note for this episode

The register is *relief, not triumph*, and every beat should be
checked against the question *does this sound triumphant?* If it
does, dial it back. Maya reading the ep-1 question back should
stage as quiet method, not as a flourish. Jules's catch should
stage as a stop, not as a revelation. Cam's correction post should
stage as craft, not as confession. The Anya goalpost move should
stage as small, not as revelation of dishonesty — Anya is not a
villain, she is a smart person with a specific blind spot, and
her defensiveness should read as recognizable and a little sad
rather than as conclusive proof of anything. The fewer-replies
observation should stage as noted, not as vindication. The
closing beats should stage as four kids sitting at the picnic
table for a beat longer than strictly necessary, and then the
scene ending without formal closure. The uncanny-not-horror
calibration is fully dormant by ep 10 and the fear has dissolved
across the back half of the season; what remains is the quiet
satisfaction of work finished. The dialog writer should resist
giving any character a *what we learned* speech — the learning
is visible in the scene's structure, and speaking it aloud would
flatten it. The season's final image is four 11-year-olds who
have done adult-ish work without anyone staging that they have
done it, a closed notebook on a picnic table, and the forest
they have walked through for the last six weeks still here.

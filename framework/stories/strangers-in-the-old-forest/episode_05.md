## Authorial notes

Episode 5 is Maya's Logic inflection and Cam's peer-audit seed in
one scene, and it earns the `strength_prevails` shape by doing the
slow work of the reversal on-page. The ep-4 weakness (shape B:
conclusion-with-feeling, articulation invisible) is not forgotten
or erased; it is worked through. Maya writes her way out of it, and
the writing is the turn. The dialog writer should treat Maya's
writing as *physical* — pen on page, visible crossings-out,
rephrasing out loud — because the physical process is the character
move. The notebook is the secondary speaker in this episode.

Three strengths and one weakness is heavy, but it is what the arc
summary points at and it is what the shape demands. The two Maya
strengths and the Cam strength are fundamentally *one chain of
strength* viewed from two angles: Cam notices something, Maya
receives it and revises, the chain continues and closes. Splitting
the single chain into three facet-level strengths serves the
coverage contract (`inferential_validity` and `internal_consistency`
for Maya; the peer-audit seed for Cam) without fragmenting the
scene's lived experience, which is one sustained collaborative
move.

On Jules as the sole weakness carrier: the `strength_prevails`
shape needs some friction for the strength to prevail against, and
Jules's residue is the most honest source of friction in this
episode. Her fear has not dissolved just because the chain has
closed. Her *could it be both?* question is on-character for her
binary-framing habit (ep 2 planted *aliens or boring*; ep 5 shows
the habit's *aliens-and-genetics* inversion) and consistent with her
engine role — she is not growing, she is asking the questions that
keep the investigation honest. The reader should feel the question
land quietly and the group sit with it rather than push it away.
That sitting-with is part of what it means for the strength to
prevail: the chain has closed, the binary-framing has not, and the
scene moves on holding both.

On Jules's facet being `inferential_validity` rather than
`sufficiency`: the move is about the structure of the inference —
she is treating the chain's silence on aliens as space where aliens
can remain, which is treating *absence of exclusion* as *absence of
conclusion*. That is an invalid inference step, not a quantity-of-
evidence issue. `sufficiency` was her ep-1 mirror form (*too little*
evidence extended into a causal story); this is a different error
about link-quality between premises. The facet choice matters for
coverage (sufficiency landed in ep 1 and ep 3; inferential_validity
in ep 2) and the facet here continues Jules's ep-2 pattern rather
than reprising ep 1.

On Cam's strength facet being `internal_consistency` rather than
`source_credibility`: the move is *noticing that a claim made
earlier does not align with a claim made later* — on a friend's
on-page writing, not on an external source. That is the textbook
shape of internal_consistency (claims failing to align across a
piece of extended reasoning). Calling it source_credibility would
force the move into an external-source frame that the ep-8 Anya
call handles more precisely. Cam does not yet carry
internal_consistency as a declared facet anywhere else — strengths
are not bound to a carrier's weakness facets, and the move fits this
facet cleanest.

On the ep-4 crossed-out Anya line as Cam's read-back anchor: this
was the cleanest available anchor because (a) it is a *written*
artifact Cam can physically point at, (b) it is from the immediate
prior episode, so the reader does not need to reach for it, and (c)
it threads ep 4's small self-correction forward into ep 5's peer
audit, which makes the two episodes feel like one continued arc
rather than two disconnected beats. The design doc's arc summary
says *a sentence Maya wrote in the notebook on Monday*, but Monday's
notebook writing was a question and a half-objection fragment —
neither available as a claim to contradict. The ep-4 crossed-out
line serves the arc's functional purpose (an earlier written line
that misfits current writing) in the cleanest form the chain of
drafts has produced.

On Priya's role and the ep-6 absence planting: Priya is present in
this episode in a supportive-but-not-target-carrying capacity. She
supplies a couple of remembered words from Ms. Reyes's class
(*phenotype*, *alleles*), she nods at Maya's revised line, and she
is the one who proposes plotting sightings on a map. The map-
suggestion is load-bearing for ep 6 and needs to come from Priya
specifically, not from Cam or Maya, because it sets up the scope
problem that Priya's presence-then-absence makes structural. Her
off-hand mention of a family thing Saturday is the cleanest way to
plant her ep-6 absence without dramatizing it; the reader should
register it and move on, the way the characters do. Dramatizing her
absence would telegraph the ep-6 scope beat; understating it lets
ep 6's absence do its work as structure rather than as event.

No social dynamics in this episode. `group_pressure` (friend-
protective) does not surface because the group is not pressing on
Jules in a way that would require anyone to soften — the scene is
collaborative, the disagreement is gentle, and Jules's residue-
question is answered carefully rather than dismissed or pushed.
`authority_deference` is dormant: Anya is present in this episode
only as the crossed-out artifact of yesterday's self-correction,
not as an invoked authority. Adding a social dynamic here would
over-engineer the scene, and the `strength_prevails` shape lands
cleaner with the cognitive layer carrying the work.

## Why these targets

**inferential_validity / Jules / black_and_white_thinking.** The
design doc commits Jules to `inferential_validity` and
`black_and_white_thinking` as paired across the season. Episode 2
planted the pattern in its *or* form (*aliens or boring*). Episode
5 surfaces its *and* form (*genetics or genetics-and-aliens*),
which is the same binary-framing habit rotated 90 degrees: rather
than picking one side, Jules is building a frame in which both sides
can coexist so she does not have to drop the interesting one. That
rotation is important for the arc because Jules does not have a
growth beat — her pattern must stay live across the season, and the
reader must feel that it is the same pattern in different episodes
rather than a new pattern each time. The facet is
`inferential_validity` because the move concerns whether the chain
*implies* a mundane cause is sufficient; Jules is treating absence
of exclusion as absence of conclusion, which is an invalid
inference. This does not contradict the chain's content; it
mis-structures the logical consequence.

**inferential_validity (strength) / Maya.** The design doc names
ep 5 as Maya's inflection episode for this facet: *"In episode 5
she writes the chain out step by step in the notebook for the
first time in her life — clunky and slow."* The strength beat is
the externalization itself — not the content of the chain, but the
act of writing a chain where each step can be pointed at. The
reader should feel that Maya has done something she could not do
yesterday, and that the chain closes because she did it, not
because the content is easy. Pairing this strength with the ep-4
weakness on the same facet (across two episodes, not within one)
is the cleanest two-beat arc for her Logic growth.

**internal_consistency (strength) / Maya.** The design doc commits
Maya to `internal_consistency` as her third Logic facet, and ep 5
is the natural home for it: this is the first time the notebook is
active across multiple days with Maya's own writing in it. The
revision move — catching the drift between yesterday's crossed-out
line and last night's new line, and rewriting without defense — is
the textbook shape of catching one's own internal consistency.
It is small, quick, and delivered without fuss, which is the right
texture for a planting beat.

**internal_consistency (strength) / Cam.** The design doc describes
this as *"a small expansion of his Evidence arc into auditing
statements, not just online sources."* Cam's carried weakness
facets (`source_credibility`, `source_diversity`, `sufficiency`)
do not include `internal_consistency`, and strengths are not bound
to weakness facets. Calling this move `internal_consistency` rather
than `source_credibility` is correct because the target of the
audit is an internal on-page claim (Maya's own earlier writing),
not a credentialed source. The seed is small — Cam does not do the
move unprompted on Anya here — and the inflection is ep 8; ep 5
plants the habit on the easiest available surface (a friend's page,
pointed to by physical proximity) so ep 8's harder application
(an off-screen credentialed adult) has a seed to grow from.

## Calibration note for this episode

The register shifts again. Episode 3 was the alien-anxiety peak;
episode 4 was the Logic weakness plant; episode 5 is the first
episode where *something happens* that moves the investigation
forward. The reader should feel that forward motion, but should
also feel that the strength is *earned*, not *awarded*. Maya is
not transformed; she has written one chain, once, slowly, with a
friend's help. The dialog writer should render her writing as
effortful — not heroic — and should let the strength land as a
small and real thing rather than a turning-point moment. Cam's
peer-audit should feel like *a friend paying close attention*,
not *a critic running down a checklist*. Jules's residue-question
should feel *tired and not ready*, not *back-sliding* or *in
denial*. Priya's mention of Saturday should be off-hand — the kind
of logistics aside 11-year-olds drop into conversations — not a
dramatic setup. The uncanny-not-horror-adjacent calibration still
applies: Jules's fear is quieter in this episode than in episode 3
but is not gone, and the careful reader should feel it beneath her
*could it be both?* question.
